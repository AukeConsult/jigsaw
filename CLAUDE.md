# Karins jigsaw – project guide

Karins jigsaw lets a person make a jigsaw puzzle from any picture and put it together on the screen, alone or with others: users, rounds (one game of a jigsaw by one user), timing, and a guide in nine languages. Everything saves by itself. Built on the same structure as Seven Friends.

## Structure
- `frontend/` – Angular 20 (standalone components, signals, Bootstrap 5 + SCSS, Transloco), hosted on Firebase Hosting site `karins-jigsaw` (target `jigsaw`, web app `karins-jigsaw`). The app opens with Karins jigsaw.
- `functions/` – Node 22 + TypeScript Express backend, deployed as ONE Cloud Function, `jigsawApi`, in the codebase `jigsaw` (served at `/jigsaw/api/**`: the jigsaws, their pieces and pictures, the users and the plays; code in `functions/src/jigsaw/`, `createJigsawApp`). What the routes share is in `functions/src/common/` (the list of documents `jigsaw-list.ts`, the document store, the allowed origins). The frontend calls it through `environment.jigsawApi`; Hosting (`firebase.json`) and the dev proxy (`frontend/proxy.conf.json`) send `/jigsaw/api` to the function.
- `functions/src/models/shared/` – **the model, the ONE place to edit shared code.** `scripts/sync-models.mjs` copies it to `frontend/src/app/models/shared/` (generated, gitignored, never edit that copy). Shared files may only import from each other.
- `client-wrappers/android` – Capacitor Android shell, generated with `npm --prefix frontend run cap -- add android` (not generated yet).

### The model (`functions/src/models/shared/`)
A saved jigsaw is a `JigsawSpec` and is checked by `JigsawSettings`; the pieces on the board are placed objects (`PlacedFigure`) and `Puzzle` is what they do together.
| file | what is in it |
|---|---|
| `jigsaw-settings.ts` | a saved jigsaw as a document (`JigsawSpec`: name, size, grid, difficulty, line and colours, owner, visibility, timed) and `JigsawSettings`, which checks one |
| `jigsaw.ts` | the cut into pieces (`JigsawCut`), `JigsawPieces`, `pieceGrid`, limits |
| `play.ts` | a round (`Plays`, `PlaySpec`): positions, settings of the player, time played |
| `popularity.ts` | what makes a jigsaw popular (`Popularity`: rounds played and being new) |
| `jigsaw-user.ts` | the users who play (`JigsawUsers`) |
| `placed.ts`, `puzzle.ts`, `geometry.ts`, `base-object.ts` | placed objects (the pieces are placed objects), `Puzzle`: what they do together (snap, layers, groups, collecting pieces), points, boxes and the part of a picture a frame shows, the settings of an object (`BaseObject`) |

### The app and its routes
The new front page is the top route **`/`** (`jigsaw/jigsaw-front/`, inside `JigsawShell`, in `app.routes.ts`: a welcome, the user's open rounds, and the finder where jigsaws are looked for and picked: the front page shows the jigsaws, the newest first (`app-jigsaw-finder`): all of them, or those that match what was looked for, with a line that says so and a way to change the search or to look at all again; looking is the **Find** page in the menu, `/jigsaw/find` (`jigsaw-find/`: only the switch of cards or list and the choices of category, sub-category, size, difficulty and owner, and a Go button, which keeps them under `FRONT_FINDER_KEY` (`jigsaw.front`, in `jigsaw-finder/finder-preferences.ts`) and goes to the front page). There is no list page, and the old `/jigsaw/list` goes to `/`); any address that is not known goes to `/`. The rest is the route tree `/jigsaw` (`app/jigsaw/`: `jigsaw-shell.ts`, `jigsaw.routes.ts`), loaded when it is first opened (`loadChildren`). The older front page (`jigsaw-home/`) is kept at `/jigsaw`. The shell is the header menu; the root (`app.ts`) only holds the router outlet.

### The frontend (`frontend/src/app/`)
- `jigsaw/` – the pages and what only the jigsaw uses: `jigsaw-front/` (the front page, at `/`: the welcome, the user's open rounds (`my-rounds/`) and the finder), `jigsaw-home/` (the older front page at `/jigsaw`, with the photo `public/images/front.jpg`), `jigsaw-find/` (the Find page), `jigsaw-finder/` (**finding a jigsaw**: `app-jigsaw-finder` is the filter, the count, the cards or rows and Show more, with its choices kept on the device under its `storageKey`; use it wherever a jigsaw is picked) and `jigsaw-filter/` (`app-jigsaw-filter`: all the choices shown to tick, a text, the categories (and under each ticked one its sub-categories), the sizes, the difficulties and the owners, one or more of each, bound as `[(filter)]`; its rules are `JigsawFilters` in `models/shared/jigsaw-filter.ts`), `view-switch/` (`app-view-switch`, cards or list), `jigsaw-card/`, `jigsaw-row/`, `category-picker/` (sets the categories of a jigsaw) and `category-tags/` (shows them), `jigsaw-page/` (one jigsaw and its rounds), `jigsaw-new/` (the form to make and edit), `jigsaw-play/` (a round), `jigsaw-user/` (who plays), `puzzle-piece/` (draws a piece), `picture-cropper/` (finds the cut of a picture), `fit-picture.ts` (cuts the picture to the shape), `piece-graphics.ts` (draws the graphic of each piece), `piece-sync.ts` (keeps the pieces in the database) and the API services `jigsaw.service.ts`, `jigsaw-image.service.ts`, `jigsaw-pieces.service.ts`, `plays.service.ts`, `jigsaw-user.service.ts`.
- `board/` – the board the pieces are played on: `jigsaw-board/` (`JigsawBoard`: the picture area, the store of pieces and all pointer tools), `board-shape/` (`BoardShape`: draws a shape as SVG) and `drag.ts`.
- `guide/` – the user guide page. `core/` – `autosave.ts` (saves a change a pause after it), `api-collection.ts` (the base of the API services), `session-storage.ts` (choices kept for the session), `theme.ts` (`cssVar`, `cssNumber`, `isWideScreen`: code that draws on a `<canvas>` element reads colours and sizes from the look), `i18n.ts`, `language.service.ts`, `user-header.interceptor.ts` and the Transloco files.
- `src/styles.scss` – **the one place for the look**: fonts, colours, sizes, layers and every custom class, as `--app-*` tokens on `:root` (Bootstrap's own variables are set from them) and responsive rules (`from`, `below`, `touch` mixins; breakpoints `$bp-*`). Components use Bootstrap classes and the classes/tokens of this file: no hard-coded colour, font or size in a template, an SVG attribute, drawing code or a component `styles:`. Code that draws on a `<canvas>` element reads tokens with `cssVar`/`cssNumber`.
- `models/shared/` – the model (generated copy, never edited).
- Backend: `common/jigsaw-list.ts` (the routes of the list of jigsaw documents: they only check, call `stored()` and the store), `common/document-store.ts` (the Firestore code: list, add, replace, delete).

## Rules for the code
1. **Classes work on their own object.** A rule belongs on the class that owns the data, at the right level of the parent/child hierarchy: what every object has is on `BaseObject`, where a piece is and what its place means on `PlacedFigure`, what the pieces do together on `Puzzle`. A new kind of object overrides small hooks instead of adding `if (kind === …)` branches. Placed objects know their own geometry; the puzzle does what they do together.
2. **Model objects never change.** Moving or any other change gives a new object (or a new puzzle, or the same one when nothing changed). Components keep the plain stored data in a signal and make the classes from it when they need them (`Puzzle.from(...)`).
3. **Components are thin.** A component turns what the person does into calls on the model classes and shows the result. Business rules do not live in components or in the routes.
4. **What is stored is decided by the model.** `X.stored(...)` says which fields are saved (only the known ones); `X.fromStored(...)` reads a document that an older version saved and gives it what it lacked. Routes only call them. Never save a field that is not known.
5. **One validation, used everywhere.** `error()` on the classes (`BaseObject.of(data).error()`, `JigsawSettings(...).error()`) is used by the forms, the page and the API, so they cannot disagree.
6. Keep files small and about one thing; no unused code (check with `tsc --noUnusedLocals`); comments say *why* in plain words.
7. Always use Bootstrap in pages; custom CSS only if absolutely necessary, and then in `styles.scss` (inline styles only where a value is computed, such as a position or size from the data).
8. Never edit anything in a `lib/` or `dist/` directory (compiled output): edit the `.ts` source and rebuild.
9. Do not guess when debugging: establish the mechanism (exact line/condition) before fixing; if it is not found, ask for evidence.
10. **Stored pixels, drawn at a scale.** Sizes and positions in the data are stored pixels and never depend on the screen. The board draws the pieces area at `scale` (`jigsaw-board.ts`: the picture and the space round it fitted to the room, whole and in proportion, at most 1, at least 0.1), and every pointer position is turned back with `/ scale()` before it reaches the model. The store frame is beside the picture, or below it on a screen narrower than `--app-store-below` (1200 px, `stacked`); this is only how it is drawn: the store is then a short strip (`STACKED_STORE_ROWS` grid steps high) that scrolls sideways only, the tall store cut into bands side by side; `place()` and `areaPoint()` map between a stored place and its place in the strip. Beside the picture the frame scrolls up and down only: a store wider than the frame is shown in slices one under the other (`sliceCount`), the same kind of mapping. The stored coordinates of the pieces never change. New pointer code must do the same; the handles divide their size by `--app-scale` so they stay finger-sized.
11. **Small screens.** Below 1200 px (`--app-sidebar-fold`, `$bp-xl`) the menu (`.app-navbar` of `jigsaw-shell.ts`) is at the bottom of the screen, sticky there (the shell is a column, the page `order: 1` and the menu `order: 2`; a page that fills the screen leaves room for it with `bottomBarHeight()` of `core/theme.ts`; the list of languages opens upward), and the jigsaw sidebar is folded away and opens over the board with the **Details** button in the jigsaw menu (a click anywhere but on its own controls closes it) (`JigsawHelper.sidebarOpen`, kept in the session as `jigsaw.sidebarOpen`).
12. **Every page remembers what the person last chose.** A choice of the person's own on a page (a view, a tab, a switch, a selection, a filter, where a floating frame was put, the defaults of a new jigsaw) is kept on the device in `localStorage` with `readPreference`/`writePreference` (`core/preferences.ts`; the value is checked when it is read, storage that is missing or refuses means the page starts with its default) and used when the page is opened again, also another day. Kept: the list (`jigsaw.list`: list or cards, the contributor, whether the choice of contributors is open), the user page (`jigsaw.userTab`, `jigsaw.userFilter`), the helper of a round (`jigsaw.helper`, `jigsaw.edgesOnly`, `jigsaw.helperFrame`, `jigsaw.helperScale`), the defaults of a new jigsaw (`jigsaw.newDefaults`: size, number of pieces, difficulty, timed, line and colours; not the name, picture, description or author, which belong to that jigsaw), the language (`jigsaw.language`) and who plays (`jigsaw.userId`). Not kept, on purpose: what covers the page (the open sidebar `jigsaw.sidebarOpen` is for the session only, in `sessionStorage`), the Debug and Cheat switches, and what is typed for one jigsaw. A new choice on a page is kept in the same change that adds it.

## Rules for what the app does
- **A jigsaw** is a document with a name, a size in pixels (whole), a grid, a difficulty and a look, an owner and who sees it, and whether it is timed. The size, the picture, the pieces, the difficulty and whether it is timed are fixed once somebody has started a round (the service answers 409 to a change of them for everybody, the owner too, whoever has the address: `JigsawSettings.changesWhatPlayersShare` for the settings, and a guard on every route that puts in, takes away or moves the picture or the pieces; what can still change is the name, the description, the author, who sees it, and the look; the edit panel shows only those). **Who may change a jigsaw:** only its owner (the service answers 403 to anybody else for the settings, delete, and taking away or moving the picture and pieces; a jigsaw from before owners has no owner and is anybody's; the owner can hand it over by changing the author). `X-Jigsaw-User` says who asks, and with no sign-in yet this keeps the app honest, it is not a lock. **A jigsaw only its owner sees** (`visibility: "owner"`) is listed for its owner and for the players who have a round on it (also one that is only opened); they keep their rounds and go on with them, the page says it is private, and only the owner starts another round (403 to a player, 404 to anybody else, also for its rounds); its picture and graphics are plain files read by address, so they are not hidden.
- **Many jigsaws at once** (`/jigsaw/batch`, `jigsaw-batch/`, reached from the "Your jigsaws" tab of the user page, "Make many jigsaws at once"; behind `userGuard`): the person chooses the number of pieces (24 to 600), the width of the picture (600 to 1200 px), the difficulty, whether it is timed, the language of the names and descriptions (the language the app is shown in to begin with) and whether the AI sets the categories, name and description (on to begin with: the pictures are sent to OpenAI, and the page says so); then **many pictures at once from the file system** (`<input type="file" multiple>`, jpeg, png or webp, at most `BATCH_MAX` = 30). "Make n jigsaws" makes them one after the other: `JigsawMaker` (`jigsaw-maker.ts`) cuts the picture to its own shape (the picture area is `width` wide and as high as the picture's shape says), cuts it into pieces, saves the jigsaw (owner: the user who plays, author: their name), uploads the picture and saves the pieces with their graphics, and takes the jigsaw away again when a step fails; then `JigsawService.classify(id, true)` puts the AI's categories, name and description on it. Each picture shows its state (waiting, cutting, saving, the AI looking, done or failed with why), a failed picture does not stop the others, the AI failing leaves the jigsaw made (it says why), and Stop ends after the picture being made. The choices (not the pictures) are kept on the device (`jigsaw.batch`).
- **Deleting many of your jigsaws at once** ("Your jigsaws" tab of the user page, `jigsaw-user/`): each row has a checkbox, "Select all" ticks or clears them all (half-ticked when only some are), and "Delete the selected" asks first ("Yes, delete n" / Cancel) and then deletes them at the same time (`Promise.allSettled` over `JigsawService.removeAs`, so only the owner's succeed); the ones that could not be deleted stay ticked and the page says how many. The row checkbox is `position-relative z-2` because the row's `stretched-link` overlay (z-index 1) would otherwise cover it and swallow the click. The ticks are for the moment only (not kept).
- **Public, private, hidden** (`JigsawSpec.visibility`: `everyone` (public, what none says), `owner` (private, above) or `hidden`; the rules are `JigsawVisibilities.restricted` (private and hidden: only the owner and the players with a round open it, 404 to others) and `.listed` (not hidden), used by the routes). **A hidden jigsaw is only in the list "Your jigsaws" of its owner** (`GET /jigsaw/api/jigsaw`): never in the search, the front page, the popular ones, the counts, not even for its owner, and its page is opened by its address. Private and hidden need an owner. Chosen in the select "Who sees it" on the forms of a jigsaw, and for many at once on the user page: tick jigsaws and use the select "Who sees it…" beside "Delete the selected" (each ticked jigsaw is saved at the same time as it is, with the new visibility; `JigsawSettings(...).stored()`); a row shows a lock or an eye-slash when it is private or hidden. A welcome ("Welcome back, name") on the front page is shown only when the person was last active more than an hour ago (`core/activity.ts`, `Activity.awayLong`, worked out once when the app opens). The page of the user is wider (`.form-card.wide`). The button "Enter a round, solve it" continues the user's newest unsolved round when there is one. Pictures cannot be dragged away (`img { -webkit-user-drag: none }`).
- **Classify from the edit panel** (page of a played jigsaw, "Basics" section): the link "Let the AI set categories, name and description" calls `JigsawService.classify(id, true)` (the picture is sent to OpenAI; the panel says so), puts the name, description and categories it got in the form (so the autosave that follows sends the same) and shows why when it fails (`classifyError`). The page of a jigsaw shows the categories as badges under the description (`app-category-tags` in `jigsaw-header`). The form of an unplayed jigsaw (`jigsaw-new`) has no such button yet.
- **One form for the fields of a jigsaw** (`jigsaw/jigsaw-fields/`, `app-jigsaw-fields`): the name and the AI button ("Let the AI set categories, name and description", under the name, only when it is given a saved jigsaw: `classifyId`), the description, the language, the categories, the look (line and colours), and the owner and who sees it. The form of a new jigsaw and of an edit (`jigsaw-new`) and the edit panel of a played jigsaw (`jigsaw-page`) both use it: they own the values (`[(name)]="name"` and so on, signals), what is saved and when; the picture and size go in through its default slot and the difficulty and the clock through the slot `play-settings`. The panel of a played jigsaw also has "Delete all rounds to change them" (owner only, `DELETE /jigsaw/:id/plays`), which takes the rounds away and goes on to the whole form. Its texts are the scope `jigsawNew`.
- **Easy to start (made for an average adult):** the form of a jigsaw shows only the picture, the name, the description, the size and pieces, and (when there is a choice) the difficulty and the clock; **"More options"** (`<details class="more-options">` in `app-jigsaw-fields`: language, categories, motive difficulty, look, owner and who sees it) is closed to begin with, its state is kept on the device (`jigsaw.formMore`, only when the person opens or closes it) and it is open while the author has still to be chosen. A card always tells the picture, the name, the number of pieces and the difficulty; the author, categories, motive and date are behind the Details switch, which is **on** to begin with (`CardDetails`). The menu has words under its icons on a phone or tablet (`.nav-label`). The front page's welcome band (for a person with no round going) has three steps and a link "Make a jigsaw". The button on the page of a jigsaw is "Start playing".
- **A new person only says their name** (`/jigsaw/user` starts in mode `start`: the title is "What is your name?", one field and the button "Start playing"; `JigsawUserService.createFromName` makes the user with a user name worked out from the name, `JigsawUsers.usernameFrom(name, digits)`: the plain name first, four digits added only when the server answers 409 (taken), never for another error (a taken user name makes nothing: `createFromName` says which user name, `lookup` reads that user and the page asks). When somebody already has the name (or its user name) the page first asks "Is that you?" (`JigsawUser.sameUsers`, a list to choose from when several have the name): "Yes, that is me" plays as that user, "No, make a new user" makes one with digits). The list of the users is behind "I have played before" (so nobody presses a name that is not theirs) and the whole form (user name, language, e-mail) behind "Choose my own user name".
- **The form of a jigsaw takes one place in the history** (so Back does not need pressing twice): Edit on the page of a jigsaw, and Save and Cancel of the form, move with `replaceUrl: true` (the page is replaced by the form, the form by the page), and so does the making of a new jigsaw (the form is replaced by the page of the new jigsaw: **no round is started**; "Save" only saves it, "Save and analyse with AI" also has the AI set its categories, motive difficulty, name and description and then never leaves the page: the same page becomes the edit form of the new jigsaw in place (`JigsawNew.editIdState`, the address is replaced with `Location.replaceState`), with what the AI said in it, to look at and change). **The form is in this order:** Picture (with the AI button under it once one is chosen), Basics (name, description, language, categories, motive difficulty), Size and pieces, Play and look, Owner and sharing (`app-jigsaw-fields` has a slot `[picture]` at its top for it; `pictureFirst`) and a redirect away from the form.
- **Jigsaws are read a page at a time, and one by one.** The finder (`app-jigsaw-finder`) asks for 20 jigsaws (`pageSize`, `limit=20&page=n`) and adds the next 20 when an invisible mark under the list comes within 400 px of the screen (endless scroll: an `IntersectionObserver` in an `effect`, one page at a time; a browser without it gets the button "Show more"). A changed filter starts again at page 1. **One jigsaw is read by its id** (`GET /jigsaw/api/jigsaw/:id`, `ApiCollection.get`, null for 404 or a jigsaw the person may not see; the store's `get` reads the document itself, so an old jigsaw is found: `list()` is only the newest 100): the page of a jigsaw, a round, the edit form and the rounds band of the front page (each open round's jigsaw once, `MyRounds`) no longer read the whole list. Not yet paged: the rounds of a user over all jigsaws (`userPlaysRouter` asks every jigsaw of that list). The form does not say that a name is missing while the jigsaw is still being read.
- **"Your jigsaws" (user page) is read a page at a time too, with words, an order and who sees them chosen on the server** (a user can have up to 500 jigsaws): `GET /jigsaw/api/jigsaw-search/mine?text=&sort=&visibility=&limit=&page=` (`JigsawSearch.mine`, in `functions/src/jigsaw/jigsaw-search.ts`; 401 without `X-Jigsaw-User`; 400 for a visibility or a sort that is not allowed) gives the jigsaws of the user who asks, the private and the hidden ones too, never another user's, as `{items, total, hasMore, counts}` where `counts` is how many there are for All, Public, Private and Hidden with the words kept. The sorts are `new`, `old`, `name`, `pieces` (fewest first) and `most`. The page (`jigsaw-user/`) asks for 25, adds the next 25 with "Show more" ("Showing n of m"), starts again from the first page when the words (after 0.3 s), the order, who sees them or the user change, and keeps the words, the order and the choice on the device (`jigsaw.userJigsawText`, `jigsaw.userSort`, `jigsaw.userVisibility`). The list has only what a list needs (no objects), so a change (who sees it, Recommended) first reads the whole jigsaw (`changed()`), since a save replaces the whole document. "Select all" ticks what is shown.
- **Cards and rows can show or hide their details** (the author, the categories, the pieces, the difficulty and the date): the switch "Details" beside Cards / List (`app-view-switch`, `CardDetails` in `jigsaw/card-details.ts`, kept on the device as `jigsaw.cardDetailsOn`, on to begin with) is one choice for every list. The name on a card has its own size (`--app-fs-compact`, class `.card-name`, 11 px on a phone), and the tiny size starts at 9 px. The quick filters (All, Favorites, Recommended) each switch on and off: taken off, the user's own search from the Find page is looked for again (all the jigsaws when there is none), and the quick filter for the search is an icon that is always there. The Find page has Go at the top as well.
- **What the AI uses is kept for the user who pays** (token use, to be paid for later): each call of the classification (`POST /jigsaw/:id/classify`) that the AI answered keeps one document in the subcollection `jigsaw-users/<uid>/token-usage/<id>` (`JIGSAW_TOKEN_USAGE`, `TokenUsageSpec`, `models/shared/token-usage.ts`; `functions/src/jigsaw/token-usage.ts`, `TokenUsageStore`; the user is the one in `X-Jigsaw-User`, else the owner): `provider`, `model` (asked for) and `responseModel`, `requestId` (OpenAI's id of the call), `inputTokens`, `outputTokens`, `totalTokens`, `cachedInputTokens`, `reasoningTokens`, `imageDetail`, `kind` (`classify`), `jigsawId`, `status` (`ok`, or `failed` when the answer could not be used: the tokens are spent all the same, `ClassifierFailed.usage`), `applied` (put on the jigsaw), `createdAt` (ms) and `date` (ISO text), and `billed` (false until a payment takes it in). `TokenUsages.fromOpenAi` reads OpenAI's `usage`, `record` makes the document, `totals` adds up (also what is not yet billed). A call that could not be asked (503) or was refused (403) keeps nothing; keeping never fails the classification. The user reads their own with `GET /jigsaw/api/jigsaw-users/:id/token-usage` (`{items, totals}`, 403 to anybody else). The classification the page gets does not carry the use. No price is kept (prices change: work it out from the tokens and the model when billing).
- **AI classification of one jigsaw** (OpenAI; `POST /jigsaw/api/jigsaw/:id/classify`, `?apply=true` to save): the AI looks at the stored picture and says its **categories, a name and a description**, the texts written in the language of the jigsaw. Only the owner may (403 otherwise; 404 no jigsaw, 409 no picture, 503 when the settings are not there, 502 when OpenAI fails). Without `apply` nothing is saved; with it the categories (when it said some), the name and the description (when it said one) are put on the jigsaw and nothing else of it changes. **The motive difficulty** is a property of a jigsaw, like its difficulty, and not a category: `JigsawSpec.motive` is one of `easy`, `mediumeasy`, `normal`, `hard`, `expert` (`models/shared/motive.ts`, `JigsawMotives`; how hard the picture itself is to put together, from how easy it is to tell its parts apart: **a picture with few distinct details (a sky, a calm sea, one colour, a repeating pattern) is harder than one with many different details**, because the pieces look alike; **the AI does not choose the level: it gives three ratings (`detail` and `colour`, each 1-5, and `landmark`, true or false) and the level is worked out from them by `JigsawMotives.fromRatings`: detail is how many different shapes, objects and textures, colour is contrast and colour (how strongly the parts differ in brightness and colour); the two add up: 9-10 easy, 7-8 medium easy, 5-6 normal, 4 hard, and 2-3 (almost no detail and almost no contrast or colour) expert, except that something that clearly stands out (a mark, a line, an object: `landmark`) makes that hard, for example a white check mark on a blue wall; the AI is told to give the lower number when in doubt; the five levels and their words are unchanged**; not from the number of pieces; none until somebody says it). It is stored, read (`fromStored` also takes a `motive/hard` that an older version kept among the categories and makes it the property) and checked by `JigsawSettings`; it is not one of the things every round shares, so it can change at any time. The answer has the three ratings (the schema has no `motive` field; `Classifications.read` works the level out, and still takes a `motive` said outright when there are no ratings) and the route puts the level on the jigsaw with `apply`. It is found like the difficulty: `motives` in the search, the filter (`JigsawFilter.motives`) and the counts (`JigsawIndex.motive`, `motive` is a summary field); a jigsaw with none is never found by it. The form has a select (`app-jigsaw-fields`, `jigsawNew.motive`), and cards, rows, the page and the list of the user show it beside the difficulty with the icon `MOTIVE_ICON`. The texts are `jigsawLevels.motive.*`. The model class is `Classifications` (`models/shared/classification.ts`): the strict JSON schema with the category keys as the only values, the prompt (the categories, the limits, the language), and `read`/`tidyCategories` which check what comes back (only categories that exist, at most three, a sub-category instead of its category, "other" only when alone; texts tidied and cut to `JIGSAW_MAX_NAME` and `JIGSAW_MAX_DESCRIPTION`). `Classifier` is the port (`functions/src/jigsaw/classifier.ts`): `openAiClassifier` calls the Chat Completions API with `fetch` (the picture as a data address at `OPENAI_IMAGE_DETAIL`), `configuredClassifier` reads the settings when asked, and tests use a fake: **no test calls OpenAI**. **The settings are in the top-level `/.env`** (git-ignored; the template is `.env.example`): `OPENAI_API_KEY` (a *project* key; never an admin key), `OPENAI_MODEL` (required, no default), `OPENAI_IMAGE_DETAIL` (low by default), `OPENAI_ORG_ID`, `OPENAI_PROJECT_ID`. `scripts/sync-env.mjs` (`npm run sync:env`, run before every functions build; `--check`, `--soft`) copies them: the settings that are not secret to `functions/.env` (the emulator reads it and a deploy takes it), **the key only to `functions/.secret.local`** (the emulator only, never deployed, a deployed function gets its key from the Firebase secret `JIGSAW_OPENAI_API_KEY`, declared on `jigsawApi` (`defineSecret`; the name is the jigsaw's own because the project is shared). **`npm run deploy` and `deploy:functions` run `sync:env` and then `scripts/deploy-secrets.mjs` first**, which puts the key of `/.env` in that secret (on the standard input of `firebase functions:secrets:set`, never on a command line or in a log) and stops when the key is missing; `.secret.local` has the key under both names, since the emulator reads a declared secret by its name; `openAiSettings` takes either). Never put a key in the repo, a file that is deployed, or a chat. `npm run classify:dummies` shows what the AI would say for 3 dummy jigsaws (`--count N`, `--all`), and `-- --apply` saves it; each picture is sent to OpenAI.
- **Favorites:** a user can tick a jigsaw with a small heart over the top right corner of its picture on the card (and a heart button on the row; none without a user who plays). A favorite is **one document for each user and jigsaw in its own collection `jigsaw-favorites`** (`models/shared/favorite.ts`: `JIGSAW_FAVORITES`, `JigsawFavorites.idOf(userId, jigsawId)` = `userId~jigsawId`, so ticking twice is the same document and taking away is deleting it; `userId`, `jigsawId`, `createdAt`). `functions/src/jigsaw/jigsaw-favorites.ts`: `GET /jigsaw/api/jigsaw-favorites` gives `{ids}` of the user who asks (the header `X-Jigsaw-User`; 401 without, 404 for a user that does not exist), `PUT /:jigsawId` ticks (404 for a jigsaw that is not there or that is private to someone else) and `DELETE /:jigsawId` takes away; a user's favorites are asked for by one equality on `userId` (no composite index); the favorites of a jigsaw that is deleted go with it. `FavoritesService` reads them once for each user who plays and changes a heart at once (put back when the server could not keep it). **The quick filter "Favorites"** (a heart button beside All and Recommended on the line of the front page, only when the user has favorites) looks only at them: the search takes `favorites=true` (`JigsawQuery.favorites`, `JigsawFilter.favorites`), and the route keeps only the jigsaws in the favorites of the user who asks (none for nobody), for the list and for the counts.
- **A jigsaw is not played twice by the same user:** once a round of theirs is solved, `POST /jigsaw/api/jigsaw/:id/plays` answers 409 for them (others may still play it), `POST .../plays/:playId/restart` answers 409 for a solved round, the page of the jigsaw shows "You have solved this jigsaw" instead of the Start button (`JigsawPage.solvedByMe`) and a solved round has no Reset round button; deleting the solved round is the way to play it again. "Start my own round" (looking at another player's round) is `JigsawPlay.startOwn`.
- **Solved:** a card shows a small green check in the top right corner of its picture (left of the heart) when the user who plays has solved the jigsaw (`SolvedService`, the ids of the jigsaws with a solved round of the user, read when the user is chosen and when the front page opens), and a round that is solved is painted light green in the lists of rounds (`.round-solved`, `--app-solved-bg`). **The quick filter "Solved"** (beside Favorites, only when the user has solved some) looks only at those: the search takes `solved=true` (`JigsawQuery.solved`, `JigsawFilter.solved`), and the route keeps only the jigsaws the user who asks has a solved round on (`solvedBy` in `app.ts`: one question for each jigsaw that is shown, 25 at a time, none for nobody), for the list and for the counts.
- **The search a user made on the Find page** (cards or list, and the filter) is kept with the user when **Go** is pressed (`JigsawUserSpec.search`, `PUT /jigsaw-users/:id {search}`, checked by `JigsawFilters.isSetup`) and on the device (`jigsaw.front`): the front page shows the jigsaws that match it. A device that has none of its own takes the user's search when the user is read, and a user who is chosen brings theirs. **A change made on the front page itself, with the quick filters (All, Recommended), is for now: it is not kept, and it does not replace the search the user made.** The finder only reads what was kept.
- **Language:** the languages are one list, `JIGSAW_LANGUAGES` (`models/shared/language.ts`: en, nb, sv, da, fi, de, fr, es, nl; `JigsawLanguages.is/of/nameOf`; the app's `LANGUAGES` is that list). **A user has a `language`** (English until chosen; set in the form on `/jigsaw/user`, made or changed with `language` on `POST`/`PUT /jigsaw/api/jigsaw-users`; `JigsawUserService` shows the app in the language of the user when the user is chosen or read, and a language chosen in the menu is kept as the user's, `changeLanguage`). **A jigsaw has an optional `language`** (what its name and description are written in; English when it says nothing: `JigsawLanguages.of`): the form of a new jigsaw starts it as the language the app is shown in (the user's), and the form of an edit and the edit panel of a played jigsaw change it (it is not one of the things every round shares). It is kept for finding (`language` in `JigsawIndex`) and can be searched (`languages`, a list, in the filter).
- **Looking for jigsaws** (`GET /jigsaw/api/jigsaw-search`, `models/shared/jigsaw-search.ts`, `functions/src/jigsaw/jigsaw-search.ts`): by lists `categories` (categories and sub-categories, `nature`, `nature/forest`; a category is every jigsaw in it, up to 6), `languages` (codes of `JIGSAW_LANGUAGES`; a jigsaw without a language is English), `sizes` (in pieces: small up to 48, medium up to 150, large up to 352, huge above), `difficulties` (hard, medium, easy) and `owners` (user UIDs, up to 10), written with commas, and a free `text` (words in the name, the author or the description, any order and case, a part of a word is enough, at most 60 characters; the description is read to look in it and is not sent on), with `sort` (new, name, pieces), `limit` and `page`; the answer is `{items, total, hasMore}` of summaries (`JigsawSummary`: no `placed`). **Each list means "any of these", and the lists and the text together mean "and".** `GET /jigsaw-search/facets` (the same parameters) counts the jigsaws for each choice with the rest of the filter kept and that list left out, so a tick says what ticking one more gives (`JigsawSearch.facets`, `JigsawFacets`: the total, the categories and sub-categories, sizes, difficulties and languages that jigsaws have, and the owners by name; the Find page asks it at each choice and shows the counts beside each tick, the languages that are there, and who made the jigsaws). **How the jigsaws are read:** `store.load(owners, 500)` makes these Firestore queries **at the same time** (`Promise.all`), each with `select` of the summary fields (`SUMMARY_FIELDS`) and a limit of `SEARCH_MAX_READ` (500): the newest jigsaws overall (`orderBy createdAt desc`, which also gets the ones with no owner), and **up to 500 of each owner's** (`where ownerUid ==`, the owners are the users). The answers are put together (each jigsaw once, the newest first) and **everything else is done in memory on those summaries** (what matches, the counts, the order, the pages), so a search and the counts are over the same jigsaws and agree. What was read is kept for 10 seconds (and forgotten when a jigsaw is made, changed or deleted), since the Find page asks at every tick. **Never an equality together with an order, a `!=` or a range in one query**: it needs a composite index, and the indexes of this shared project are not ours to deploy (the emulator does not check for missing indexes, so keep to this rule); for that reason an owner's jigsaws are asked for without an order, so an owner with more than 500 jigsaws has 500 of them read, which ones is not said. The jigsaws everybody sees and the private ones the person may see (`canSee`; only when the header says who asks) are looked at in memory too. Nothing is kept in the jigsaw documents for finding.
- **Popular jigsaws:** the front page shows up to ten (`POPULAR_SHOWN`), the best first, from `GET /jigsaw/api/jigsaw-popular`. `Popularity` (`models/shared/popularity.ts`) is the rule: a jigsaw's score is the rounds played on it (a round nobody has moved a piece in is not counted) plus a bonus for being new, 5 on the day it is made and halved every 14 days, so a new jigsaw nobody has played yet can still be seen; the same score puts the newer jigsaw first. Only the jigsaws the person may see are ranked (the same `canSee` as the list).
- **Position:** a piece's *centre* is what sticks to a grid point; a piece close to its own point clicks into place by the difficulty. Everything stays inside the picture area and the store.
- **Layers:** pieces are on layers 1–1000; a higher layer is in front.
- **Saving:** every change saves by itself a pause later (autosave); nothing is saved while the settings are not allowed. A saved change **replaces the whole document** (only the creation time is kept), so nothing removed or stale is left in the database.

## Shared Firebase project (IMPORTANT)
The Firebase project `sevenfriends` is shared with Seven Friends. Therefore:
- Deploy only `hosting:jigsaw` and `functions:jigsaw` (the functions codebase; the npm `deploy*` scripts do; `npm run deploy:jigsaw` is the site alone). Never `firebase deploy` everything, and never deploy `firestore`/`storage` rules or indexes from here: that would overwrite Seven Friends' live ones. `firestore.rules` and `storage.rules` here are for the emulator only. Change live rules/indexes from the Seven Friends repo.
- The functions codebase is `jigsaw`, not `default`, so a deploy here never deletes Seven Friends' functions.
- Collections (`jigsaw`, `jigsaw-users`) and Storage paths (`jigsaw/...`) are the jigsaw's own, so they cannot collide with Seven Friends' data.
- Do not run any `firebase` command that changes the shared project, and do not deploy, commit or push, unless asked.

## The jigsaw board and its page
The jigsaw module's page (`/jigsaw`) is called **Karins jigsaw** (the menu link, the title of the browser tab and the title at the top of its sidebar; the address `/jigsaw` and the collection `jigsaw` stay as they are). The pieces are put together on the **picture area** of the board (`JigsawBoard`); a rule below that says "the jigsaw" is about the saved document and its page, and one that says "the picture area" is about the part of the board the picture is on.
- Stored in the Firestore collection `jigsaw` through `/jigsaw/api/jigsaw` (same checks and shape as `jigsaw-list`; `JIGSAW` in the model, `JigsawService` in the frontend).
- **Where a jigsaw is kept:** its settings and its non-piece objects are the Firestore document `jigsaw/<id>` (collection `jigsaw`, `/jigsaw/api/jigsaw`). **Each piece is a document of its own** in the subcollection `jigsaw/<id>/pieces/<piece id>` (`JIGSAW_PIECES`, `JigsawPiece` in the model, `functions/src/jigsaw-pieces.ts`) with its `number` (the grid point it belongs on: row × columns + column + 1), `col`, `row`, its four edges, `size`, where it is (`centerX`, `centerY`), its `layer` and **`matched`** (it lies on its own place: the server works this out from where the piece is, `JigsawPieces.matched`, whatever the page says). The page does not put the pieces in the jigsaw document (`placed` there holds only what is not a piece). The background picture and the **graphics of the pieces are files in Firebase Storage**, `jigsaw/<id>/picture` and `jigsaw/<id>/pieces/<piece id>`.
- **Files go through the API, never from the page to Storage** (the way Seven Friends keeps its files): the page sends bytes to the API, the Cloud Function writes them with `firebase-admin` (`getStorage().bucket()`; the Admin SDK ignores the storage rules, which let nobody in, so the rules are not touched), the type is found from the first bytes (never from what the page says) and a file is read back through the API. The routes: `PUT/GET/HEAD/DELETE /jigsaw/api/jigsaw-image/:id` (the picture: the raw bytes as the body, jpeg, png or webp, **at most about 5 MB**, 404 when there is no such jigsaw) and `/jigsaw/api/jigsaw-pieces/:id` (`GET` the pieces without graphics; `PUT {pieces: [...with `graphic`: a data address of a png, webp or jpeg of at most 150 KB]}` when the jigsaw is cut: all the pieces and graphics are replaced; `PATCH {moves: [{id, centerX, centerY, layer}]}` where pieces are now; `GET .../graphic/:pieceId`; `DELETE`). Deleting a jigsaw takes away its pieces, their graphics and its picture.
- **Saving the game and reinstating it:** a cut sends all the pieces with their graphics (drawn in the page from the picture, `jigsaw/piece-graphics.ts`, a webp of each piece with its tabs); after that `PieceSync` (`jigsaw/piece-sync.ts`) saves only the pieces that were moved, a pause (0.8 s) after the last move, and the server sets `matched`. Opening a saved jigsaw reads its pieces back (`JigsawPlay`): each is where it was left, on its layer, with its edges and number, and the sidebar shows **Matched: x of y** ("solved!" when all are; no progress bar). A matched piece is drawn like every other piece, with the line of the jigsaw (no green outline; only the blink when it clicks into place is green). A jigsaw that an older version saved with its pieces in its own document has them moved to the subcollection when it is opened.
- **Background picture:** a jpeg, png or webp chosen in the panel, shown behind the grid points and objects and covering the picture area (not once it is cut: see below). Before it is saved the page **cuts it to the shape of the picture area** (width : height). Choosing a file opens `app-picture-cropper`: the picture lies behind a frame with the shape of the picture area and is dragged (and zoomed, slider or wheel, up to 4 times) to find the cut; "Use this cut" gives the part of the picture the frame shows (`PictureCrop` in the model: it never lets the frame show what is outside the picture), Cancel keeps the old picture. `fitPicture` (`jigsaw/fit-picture.ts`) makes a jpeg of that part, up to twice the size of the picture area and at most about 5 MB (`MAX_PICTURE_BYTES`). What is saved, what the pieces show and what the helper picture shows is that cut picture, so the helper picture has the shape of the picture area. If the width or height of the jigsaw is changed later the picture is not cut again (choose it again). A new jigsaw is saved when a picture is chosen, since the picture belongs to its id.
- **Helper banner and hidden picture:** once the picture is cut into pieces (the jigsaw has pieces) the picture is no longer shown behind the picture area (`backdrop` of the board: it is derived, nothing is stored; the pieces still show their parts of it). Above the jigsaw pieces area there is a one-line helper banner (`helperBanner` of the board) with a switch, "Helper picture" (on to begin with, not saved). When it is on, the whole picture is shown in a frame that floats on top of the rest of the page (`position-fixed`): it is moved by its title (`helperFrame`, kept on the window; the pointer is captured by the title, which is not redrawn), sized by its corner (`helperScale`: a quarter of the picture's width and height to begin with, from a tenth up to a half, the picture keeps its shape) and closed with its x (which turns the switch off).
- **The border round the picture area uses the piece line settings** (`JigsawSpec.lineWidth` 0-6 px and `lineColor`, the same as the line round each piece): the board draws it as an `outline` (outside the picture area, so it takes no room and nothing placed moves) when `lineWidth` is more than 0. `JigsawSpec.frameWidth` (and `JigsawSettings.DEFAULT_FRAME_WIDTH`/`MAX_FRAME_WIDTH`) is no longer set or drawn by the pages; it stays in the model so older jigsaws still load. The page for playing keeps `lineWidth`, `lineColor` and `backColor` when it saves (a save replaces the whole document).
- **The name of the picture file is kept with the jigsaw** (`JigsawSpec.pictureName`, at most `JIGSAW_MAX_PICTURE_NAME` = 200 characters): the page for a new jigsaw sends the name of the chosen file and does not show it; the page for playing sends it back when it saves (a save replaces the whole document).
- **Looking at other players' rounds:** the page of a jigsaw lists everyone's rounds (`GET /jigsaw/api/jigsaw/:id/plays`), the user's own first and marked "Yours"; another player's round opens read-only (`JigsawPlay.viewOnly`: `JigsawBoard.viewOnly` stops moving, picking and collecting, the settings, the cheat switch and the New round and Delete round buttons are not rendered (`@if (!viewOnly())`), a button starts the user's own round). The API refuses to change or delete a round unless the header `X-Jigsaw-User` is its player's UID (403; `PlaysService` sends it); there is no sign-in, so this is not a lock until there is one.
- **Font sizes: ONE scale of FIVE sizes in `styles.scss`** (tokens `--app-fs-title`, `-heading`, `-text`, `-small`, `-tiny`, each made with the `fluid(min, max)` function: the first size (rem) on a phone 375 px wide or less, the second on a screen 1400 px wide or more, a straight line between). Nothing else sets a text size: Bootstrap's `h1` and `.h1`, `h2`-`h6` and `.h2`-`.h6`, `.lead`, `.small`, `.btn`, `.form-label`, `.form-check-label`, `.form-text`, the fields, `.badge`, `.invalid-feedback` and the navbar name are set to the tokens there, and templates use `.page-title` (h1), `.section-title` (h2), `.subsection-title`, `.tiny` (dates, details), `.icon-md` instead of picking a Bootstrap heading size (`h3`, `h4`, `fs-3` ...). No component sets a `font-size` of its own (the logo is on the scale too); a size that is not one of the five is not added: use the nearest. What each is for and how big it is (phone 375 / tablet 768 / desktop 1400 px, text 16 px at least in a field so a phone does not zoom in): **title** (the page title) 22.4 / 26.1 / 32; **heading** (h2 and under, the line under a title, the name in the header, an icon beside a heading) 18 / 20.3 / 24; **text** (the text, what is typed in a field, the main button) 16 / 16.8 / 18; **small** (the label of a field and of a switch, the help and the error under a field, notes, the text of every other button) 13 / 13.8 / 15 (the label is about 80 % of the text of the fields); **tiny** (dates, details, badges, the subtitle of the logo) 9 / 9.1 / 9.3.
- **Buttons have three sizes, also a scale in `styles.scss`** (`--app-btn-*`: the text of a button is `--app-fs-small`, the main button's `--app-fs-text`; the room round the text and the gap to the icon are in `em`, so they follow the text size and a button keeps its shape; more room on a touch screen; the icon in a button is 1.15em and spaced by the gap, so a template needs no `me-1` on it): `.btn` the usual, `.btn-sm` the small one in rows and lists (also `.btn-group-sm > .btn`; it differs from the usual one by less room, not by its text), `.btn-main` (same as `.btn-lg`) the big one for the main action of a page. Never size a button in a template or a component.
- **Texts are translated (Transloco), jigsaw module only, in en, nb, sv, da, fi, de, fr, es, nl** (same way as the Seven Friends project): files `frontend/public/i18n/<lang>/<scope>.json`, scopes `jigsawHome`, `jigsawList`, `jigsawUser`, `jigsawNew`, `jigsawPage`, `jigsawPlay`, `jigsawShell`, `jigsawBoard`, `jigsawCropper`, `jigsawExample`, `jigsawLevels`, `guide`. A template declares `provideTranslocoScope('<scope>')` and `*transloco="let t"` and ALWAYS writes the English next to the key (`{{ t('jigsawHome.title') || 'Karins jigsaw' }}`); TypeScript uses `tr('scope.key', 'English')` from `core/i18n.ts`. New text: add the key to all four languages in the same change, then run `npm run check:i18n`. The language is chosen in the menu and kept in `localStorage` (`jigsaw.language`); the browser's language is NOT used (English until chosen). The guide has one file per language (`frontend/public/i18n/<lang>/jigsaw-guide.md`). Not translated: messages that come from the API or from the generated shared models. A long text must wrap inside the view, never flow outside it (check at 320 px in sv).
- **A jigsaw can be timed** (`timed` on the jigsaw: absent means timed, `false` means no clock; a switch in the form and in the edit panel of the jigsaw page). A timed jigsaw shows a "Timed" badge in the lists and on its page. In a timed jigsaw the round has a clock (`elapsedMs` on the play, saved with `PATCH .../positions`; it only grows): it counts while the round is open on the screen, is the user's own and is not solved; it shows in the menu on the left of the helper switches and in the lists of rounds. Another player looking at a round never counts.
- **A long form is grouped in sections** (`.form-section` in `styles.scss`: an `h2` with a small icon and words over a thin rule, `first` for the one at the top; used on the pages for a new jigsaw, the edit panel of a jigsaw and the forms of a user). One form: labels are plain `form-label` (not `small`), a field has `mb-3` and a row of fields `g-3 mb-3`; the sidebar of a round is the only place with small labels. Edit, Delete and the actions that change the picture are `.link-action` in a `.toolbar`, and so are the actions of a row of rounds (Continue, Look, Delete, and the question before a delete: `.link-action`, `.danger` for the delete); Cancel and Save of a form are `.btn-outline-secondary` and `.btn-primary`. New headings are new keys in all nine languages.
- **The toolbar is one standard in `styles.scss`** (a row of plain words, no boxes, no padding at the sides, so every text starts where it looks to start and lines up with what is above and below it): `.toolbar` the row (one gap size), `.segmented` a switch of choices in it (radio `btn-check` + `btn` labels; the picked one is blue, bold, underlined), `.link-action` a text that does something (icon and words; `.danger` for a delete), `.toolbar-end` pushes a part to the right end, `.toolbar-note` the tiny line under it ("3 jigsaws"), and `.link-inline` a word in a sentence that does something. Use them for every such row, now and on future pages; no `btn-link`, `px-0` or `p-0` tricks in a template.
- **Hiding in templates:** Bootstrap's `d-flex`, `d-none` and friends use `!important`, so the `hidden` attribute (or `[hidden]`) does NOT hide an element that has one of them: use `@if` (the buttons that start or delete a round were once "hidden" this way and still showed).
- **Words:** in the pages and the guide one game of a jigsaw is a **round** ("New round", "Delete round", "Your rounds"); in the code and the API it is a **play** (`PlaySpec`, `/plays`, `plays.service.ts`). The older notes here and in `api-and-code.md` that say "game" mean a round.
- **Plays** (`functions/src/models/shared/play.ts`, `functions/src/plays.ts`, `/jigsaw/api/jigsaw/:id/plays`): a jigsaw is fixed (picture, size, grid, the cut into pieces, and the defaults of difficulty and look: its `snapPercent`, `lineWidth`, `lineColor`, `backColor`); a play is one game of it by one user, identified by the user's **UID** (`userId`; never the user name, which can change) with a copy of the user's name (`userName`, put there by the server when the game starts) (`jigsaw/<id>/plays/<play id>`, positions in the one document `state/positions` as a JSON text). **A play can only choose the look** (the line and the colours; the difficulty is the jigsaw's own `snapPercent`, the same in every round, and a round follows it when the jigsaw is edited; it is shown, not chosen, in the round) (`PLAY_OVERRIDE_KEYS`; `Plays.overridesError` refuses every other key, so size, picture and pieces can never be changed by a play). The difficulty is copied into the play when it starts; the look follows the jigsaw until the play chooses its own (`Plays.effective`). A jigsaw that has plays keeps its width, height, grid, difficulty, clock (timed), picture and cut (409). **Users** (`models/shared/jigsaw-user.ts`, `functions/src/jigsaw-users.ts`, `/jigsaw/api/jigsaw-users`): there is no sign-in; a person picks their user from the list on `/jigsaw/user` (`GET /jigsaw/api/jigsaw-users`: id, user name and name by name, never the e-mail) or makes a user (a user name, 3-30 of `a-z0-9_-`, and a name); a user is a document in the collection `jigsaw-users` with a **UID** the database makes (the `id`: what a play's `userId` is), and the user name is a unique extra field used to look a user up; an optional private `email` (small letters, kept for a later connection to a Firebase registration, returned only by the POST that makes the user and never by a lookup: `JigsawUsers.publicOf`) can be given when the user is made and changed or taken away with `PUT /jigsaw/api/jigsaw-users/:id` (`{name?, username?, email?}`, only what is sent changes, the user name is checked for being taken in a transaction, the UID and so the plays never change; the form on `/jigsaw/user` has Edit) (`GET /jigsaw/api/jigsaw-users/by-username/:username`; made in a transaction so it is taken only once; a user an earlier version made has its user name as the document id and is read with it as the user name); the browser keeps the UID of the user who plays in `localStorage` (`jigsaw.userId`, `JigsawUserService`; the old key `jigsaw.username` held the id then, and is still read); a play is started only for a user that exists (`PlayDeps.userExists`, by UID). `userGuard` sends a person without a user to `/jigsaw/user?next=...` first. **Frontend:** `/jigsaw/:id` is the page of a jigsaw (`JigsawPage`: its picture and the user's games, Continue, New game, Delete, rename), `/jigsaw/:id/play/:playId` plays one game (`JigsawPlay` reads the game with `PlaysService`, saves positions through `PieceSync` with the play id, and saves the look choices as the play's `overrides` with `Autosave`; the page reloads itself when the play id in the route changes, so New game is a navigation); `Start over` is now New game, and Delete game (asked first) deletes the open game and goes to the page of the jigsaw. **The games of a user** over all the jigsaws are listed on `/jigsaw/user` (`GET /jigsaw/api/jigsaw-users/:id/plays`, `userPlaysRouter`: it asks every jigsaw for the user's plays, one equality each, so no Firestore index; a collection-group query on `plays` by `userId` would replace it, and needs a collection-group index entry that you deploy). **Owner:** a jigsaw has an optional `ownerUid` (the UID of its author, chosen from the users in a select on the form of a new jigsaw, which starts as the user who plays; the page for a jigsaw that was played has the same select); the API checks the user exists and puts the user's name in `author` itself (`stampOwner` in `app.ts`); every page that PUTs a jigsaw sends `ownerUid` back (a PUT replaces the document). **Editing:** `Edit` on the jigsaw page opens `/jigsaw/:id/edit`, the form of a new jigsaw filled in (`JigsawNew` with `editId`), while `GET /jigsaw/api/jigsaw/:id/plays/summary` says nobody has played it; saving changes the settings, and when the picture, the cut or the size changed it uploads the picture and cuts the pieces again (`saveEdit`). Once it has been played, `Edit` opens the small panel on its page (name, description, author, who sees it and the look; the difficulty and the clock are shown and cannot be changed; the server answers 409 to a change of size, grid, difficulty or clock). Not done yet: **invites** (challenge a friend on a timed jigsaw: to be planned together, first reading how the Seven Friends project does its invites and using the same methods; nothing is built yet, and the help does not mention it), real sign-in and ownership, the migration of existing jigsaws into plays (an old jigsaw is played through a new game, whose pieces are scattered anew), the front page's "continue" row from the user's games, and the collection-group index for "my plays" across jigsaws.
- **"Reset round" (it was "New round") starts the round that is open over** (it does not make another one): `POST /jigsaw/api/jigsaw/:id/plays/:playId/restart` (`Plays.restart`: the same round and player with its choices of look, the pieces scattered anew, no time played, start time now; it is a fresh round again until a piece is moved; only its player, 403 to others) and the page reads the round again (`JigsawPlay.startOver`, asked first).
- **The two helper switches are also kept for each jigsaw** (`jigsaw.helperByJigsaw`, a map of jigsaw id to `{helper, edgesOnly}`; `JigsawHelper.open(id)` is called by the round page: a jigsaw opened before shows its switches as they were left, a new one keeps the last choice). The band of the rounds going on the front page has a border in the blue, green and yellow of the pattern (`.my-rounds.ongoing`).
- **The helper choices are kept on the device** (`core/preferences.ts`, `localStorage`: kept when the page is reloaded, left and come back to, and another day; no storage, or a value that is not what it should be, means the board starts as if nothing was kept): the two switches ("Helper picture", "Only corner and edge pieces") and the place and the size of the helper picture frame (`jigsaw.helper`, `jigsaw.edgesOnly`, `jigsaw.helperFrame`, `jigsaw.helperScale`). The open sidebar of a narrow screen is kept for the session only (`core/session-storage.ts`), since it covers the board. They are not saved in the database; the Debug button is not kept.
- **The pieces are drawn in a layer of their own, on top of both frames** (the board's `overlay` and `place()`): with a store, every piece is drawn in one layer that lies over the picture area and over the store frame, and not inside either, so a piece is never cut off or put under a frame, also when it lies over the edge of one (a group of pieces that stick together can lie over the picture area and the store). The layer is as big as the whole area (so it scrolls with it), takes no room, lets the pointer through except at the pieces, and is above the frames (`z-index: 1`; the handles and the picking frame are above it). A piece of the store is put where the store frame shows it (the board follows how far the frame is scrolled, `storeScroll`), is cut off only at the top and bottom of the frame (`clip-path`, not at the sides), and is left out when it has been scrolled out of sight. Without a store the objects lie in the picture area. The frame itself is only the scrolling background and the place a frame is dragged on to pick pieces.
- **Helper: Collect pieces:** a click on the empty store frame puts the pieces that lie in the scrolling store frame together at the top of it (`Puzzle.collectPieces`, called by the board with the pieces in the store that are shown: with "only corner and edge pieces" on, the hidden ones are not collected). All the pieces of the store are taken (the whole scroll area, every band, not only what is shown). When the store scrolls up and down they are packed in rows from the top left of the frame (`COLLECT_MARGIN` from the top and the sides, `COLLECT_GAP` between, both shares of the size of a piece), in the order they were lying: from the top down, and in a row from left to right; a row that is full starts the next one. **When the store is a strip below the picture that scrolls sideways** (`stacked`) the strip is filled in the direction it scrolls (`Puzzle.collectPieces` with `across: {bandHeight, stride}`): the pieces are taken in the order they lie across all the bands (left to right, from the top down in a column) and put column by column from the left end, a column as high as a band, and when a band is full the next band goes on at the right of it. **When the store is beside the picture and wider than the frame** (it is shown in slices as wide as the frame, one under the other) the pieces are put in rows inside the first slice and, when it is full, in the next one (`down: {sliceWidth, sliceHeight}`), so no row is split over two slices; the store is made as wide as the slices the pieces took (it is only ever made wider). Pieces that stick together are put as one block and keep their places in relation to each other; a group that has a piece outside the store (or hidden) is not collected. The frame is scrolled to its top. It changes only places (they are saved like any move of pieces); the button is disabled when there is nothing in the store.
- **Helper: only corner and edge pieces:** a second switch in the helper banner, "Only corner and edge pieces" (off to begin with, not saved; disabled when there are no pieces), hides the pieces from the inside of the picture, so the border can be put together first. A border piece is one with a flat side (`JigsawPieces.isBorder`: a corner has two, an edge piece one), which is every piece with `col` or `row` on the border of the picture. What is hidden is only not drawn: it stays where it is, is saved as before, and is not picked by a frame dragged over it (`hiddenIds` of the board). The banner tells how many are shown of how many.
- **Jigsaw pieces** (`functions/src/models/shared/jigsaw.ts`, `JigsawCut`): "Cut into pieces" in the panel (when the jigsaw has a picture) replaces what is on the picture area with one piece for each whole cell of the grid (at most `JIGSAW_MAX_PIECES` = 1000 per jigsaw, so use a bigger grid for a big picture; `JIGSAW_MAX_PLACED` = 500 is for the other objects of a jigsaw). A piece is an ordinary placed square figure of grid × grid pixels with `piece` (`col`, `row` and its four edges: 1 tab, -1 blank, 0 flat on the border); a piece has no resize handles. Every inner edge is a tab on one piece and the matching blank on the other, chosen by chance. The pieces start scattered in the store frame (`JigsawCut.scatter`: in rows, with a random space between neighbours across and down from a little overlap (-0.3 of a piece) to 0.8 of a piece, `PIECE_GAP_MIN`, `PIECE_GAP_MAX`, so pieces can lie partly over each other, and each on a random layer; the store is made as wide as the pieces need). **Reset** (next to Cut into pieces, when there are pieces) shuffles all the pieces again (`JigsawCut.reshuffle`: new places and layers in the store frame, the same edges; what is not a piece stays). `app-puzzle-piece` draws a piece: its outline (`JigsawCut.path`, the tabs reach outside its square) cut out of the picture, which covers the whole picture area, so a piece shows its own cell of it wherever it lies; a matched piece has a green outline. The number of the grid point a piece belongs on is drawn on it only when **debug** is on.
- **A piece can never be lost** (`Puzzle.settled()`, used by `dropPieces` and by the board for everything it draws): every piece that is fixed (on its own place, or stuck to one that is) is on the bottom layer (`FIXED_LAYER` = 1) and every free piece is above it (layer 2 and up), so a free piece is never under a fixed one (and the pieces are drawn in a layer of their own over the picture area and the store frame, so never under them either); a free piece whose box lies outside the area is pulled back in; and a border piece is also known from its cell (`JigsawPieces.isBorder(piece, grid)`: column or row on the border), not only from its flat sides, so "only corner and edge pieces" never hides one. Old layers and places are put right when a game is drawn, nothing has to be saved for that.
- **The free space beside the picture is a place for pieces too** (`JigsawBoard.zone`, only when the store is below the picture, `stacked`, and the room beside the picture is at least three grid steps wide): a piece is either in the **store** (its centre at `centerX >= store.left`: the strip below the picture) or **free** on the board. A free piece can be anywhere in the picture area and in the empty room beside it, and moves with the pointer without a jump from the one to the other, also over the gutter above the store, and down into the store and back. Nothing new is stored: the room beside the picture stands for the stored free space under the picture (`base` = the picture height + a grid step + half a piece; `origin` is half a piece before the picture area ends), so the stored places stay within the model's area; `place()` draws such a piece beside the picture, `areaPoint()` turns the pointer there back to a stored place (for a piece, the store starts half a piece below the top edge of the strip, so a piece is whole inside the store), and the `puzzle` computed pulls a free piece that is not inside what is shown of it back in (`Puzzle.confine`), so a piece is never outside the window. A piece that is fixed on its place stays where it is. **A piece that is moved is kept on the board** (`onBoard`: not outside the pieces area, and, with the store below the picture, not below the store: dragged further down it stops at the bottom of the store and does not jump). **When the window is made smaller** and there is no room beside the picture any more, the free pieces that lay beside or under the picture are pulled up to the picture and the gutter above the store (`Puzzle.confine` in the `puzzle` computed), so none is outside what is shown. With the store beside the picture nothing of this is used (the store frame and the free space have no colour of their own: the pieces lie on the board).
- **Pieces that stick together form a group** (`Puzzle.stuckGroup`): two pieces stick when they are neighbours in the picture (next to each other, or one over the other) and lie exactly where they belong in relation to each other (one piece apart; places are whole or half pixels, so it is exact, `STICK_TOLERANCE_PX`); a piece sticks to what its neighbour sticks to, so it is a whole group. **Nothing is stored for a group: it follows from where the pieces are**, so a saved jigsaw that is opened has the same groups, and the saving of piece places (`PieceSync`) is all it needs. Picking up a piece takes its whole group with it (`moveGroup`; the board works out the pieces that move, `moving.ids`, when it is picked up); a picked frame of pieces takes the groups of all of them. **A bond is never broken** (logically or visibly): there is no way to pull a piece loose (Shift does not), a group is moved, pulled back into the area (`Puzzle.settled`) and into the free space (`Puzzle.confine`) whole, by the same amount for every piece, it is drawn by the piece that stands for it (`Puzzle.groupAnchors`, the held piece), and the server refuses (409) moves that would pull apart pieces that stick together (`Plays.bondsError`). Only Reset (all pieces are scattered anew) ends groups. **Dropping** (`Puzzle.dropPieces`) is where pieces stick: a group that is dropped within the snap range (`snapPercent`, the same setting as for clicking into place: 0 means nothing sticks) of its own grid point, or of the place where one of its pieces fits a neighbour that is not in the group, moves by the smallest of those steps, so that piece is exactly there, and the groups that now fit each other are one group. It clicks exactly or not at all: when the area has no room for the whole step the group stays where it was dropped. The pieces that joined, and the one that was dropped, blink and the device vibrates. Reset scatters all the pieces anew, and so ends every group.
- **Playing:** a piece is moved in steps 50 times smaller than the grid (`PIECE_STEP_DIVISOR`, made a whole number of half pixels, since the server only takes a whole or a half pixel for the place of a piece, and not finer than half a pixel), not from grid point to grid point, and it does not snap while it is moved. A piece that is picked up goes to the front (`Puzzle.bringToFront`: a layer above all the others) and stays there when it is dropped. **Several pieces can be picked and moved at once**: drag a frame on the empty picture area or the empty store frame (it works in both), then drag any picked piece and all move by the same amount (`Puzzle.moveGroup`: half pixels, as far as all stay inside the area; a group keeps its order when it goes to the front). **A piece clicks into place only when it is dropped** within a range of its own grid point (its home, `PlacedFigure.home()`, the centre of its cell of the picture): it goes exactly there (`Puzzle.dropPieces`, called by the board when what is moved is let go), **blinks** (swells, shakes and shines for 0.7 s: the animation is the small CSS of `app-puzzle-piece`, kept to this) and the device **vibrates** (`navigator.vibrate`, where it can). **How close it has to be is a game parameter, `snapPercent`**, a percent of the grid size, saved with the jigsaw document (`JigsawSpec.snapPercent`, a whole number from 0 to 100, where 0 means a piece never clicks; 50, half a grid step, when the jigsaw has none, `JigsawSettings.DEFAULT_SNAP_PERCENT`; `JigsawSettings.snapRangePx` makes pixels of it), (piece line: `lineWidth` 0-6 px and `lineColor` #rrggbb on the jigsaw, `JigsawSettings.DEFAULT_LINE_WIDTH/COLOR`, drawn by `.piece-outline` through `--app-line-width/-color`, and baked into the piece graphics when the jigsaw is made) set as the Difficulty (Hard 10 %, Medium 25 %, Easy 40 %; `jigsaw/snap-level.ts`) on the new jigsaw page and in the edit panel of a jigsaw (not in a round). Pieces that are in place when a jigsaw is opened or shuffled do not blink, nor do pieces that were in place when they were picked up. **A piece that lies on its own place is fixed** (`PlacedFigure.inPlace`, `Puzzle.isFixed`): it can be picked but never moved, and neither can the whole group of pieces that stick to it (`Puzzle.movable`, and `moveFigure`/`moveGroup` change nothing for them; `collectPieces` leaves them). A group with no piece on its place moves all together. Moves that wait for the 0.8 s pause are sent at once when the page is closed or hidden (`PieceSync.leaving`, a `keepalive` request), so the game is as it was left.
- **The store frame and the jigsaw pieces area (jpa):** a jigsaw that has been cut has a `storeWidth` (saved with it, `JigsawSettings.storeFrame`): a frame beside the picture area, one grid step from its right edge, `STORE_HEIGHT_FACTOR` (3) times the height of the picture area high, scrolling up and down. The cut puts the pieces in it, scattered (see below). The picture area and the frame together are the jpa, where a piece can be moved anywhere: all of it is one coordinate area (`JigsawSettings.extent`, used by the board, the page and the API to check that every object fits), and a piece with its centre right of the picture area is drawn in the frame. While a piece is moved the pointer is held by the whole board, not by the piece, since the piece is drawn again when it changes place; over the frame the pointer position is the frame's (scroll included), elsewhere it is on the part of the jpa left of the frame, and not outside what the area shows. A piece can be moved anywhere on the jpa: on the picture, in the blank space under the picture (as far down as the area shows) and in the store frame. A piece has no resize handles.
- **A jigsaw is given a number of pieces, not a grid size:** the sidebar asks for "Pieces" (2 to 1000; the standard numbers are whole columns x rows in the shape of the picture area, `JigsawCut.pieceChoices`, and picking one makes the picture area fit it exactly, `JigsawCut.layoutOf`) and tells the grid size that follows (`JigsawNew.relayout`, `JigsawCut.layoutFor`): the pieces are square, a piece is about the square root of (width × height / pieces) on a side, the grid size that gives the number of pieces nearest to the one asked for is taken, and the picture area is made a whole number of pieces wide and high (so the number can come out a few more or fewer, and the box shows the number that really comes out). It is worked out again when the width, the height or the number of pieces is changed (when the field is left), always from the size the person typed (`askedSize`), not from the size the picture area was made last time, so asking for other numbers of pieces and back gives the same picture area again. The grid size is still what is saved (`gridSize`); the number of pieces shown on opening is columns × rows. Pieces that are already cut keep the size they were cut with until the picture is cut again.
- The grid of a new jigsaw is 50 px (older documents without a grid read as 5 px).
- The jigsaw board puts its grid points half a step in from the edge (the first row and column are half a step from the border, as the last are from the far one; objects snap to those points: the centre sticks to 25, 75, 125 ... for a 50 px grid, `origin` of `Puzzle.moveFigure`), keeps its width and height a whole number of grid steps (`JigsawSettings.fittedSide`, applied when a size or the grid is changed) so the last row and column of points are as far from the far edge as the first are from the near one, numbers the points 1, 2, 3 ... row by row, and so do the pieces, but only when **debug** is on (the Debug button in the sidebar, off by default; not when the points are closer than 20 px), and has no list of objects at the side, no title, hint text or name/size line above the picture area, a **sidebar**: the settings panel (with the title "Karins jigsaw" at its top) is docked at the left edge, 17 rem wide and as high as the visible screen under the top menu (`sidebar`, measured again on resize and scroll), and the pieces area fills the rest, right under the top menu, and its **jigsaw pieces area** (jpa: the picture area with its store frame, where a piece can be moved anywhere) is as high as the visible screen, from its top to the bottom of the window (`fillScreen`, measured again on resize and scroll).
