# Karins jigsaw – project guide

Karins jigsaw lets a person make a jigsaw puzzle from any picture and put it together on the screen, alone or with others: users, rounds (one game of a jigsaw by one user), timing, and a guide in nine languages. Everything saves by itself. Built on the same structure as Seven Friends.

## Structure
- `frontend/` – Angular 20 (standalone components, signals, Bootstrap 5 + SCSS, Transloco), hosted on Firebase Hosting site `karins-jigsaw` (target `jigsaw`, web app `karins-jigsaw`). The app opens with Karins jigsaw.
- `functions/` – Node 22 + TypeScript Express backend, deployed as ONE Cloud Function, `jigsawApi`, in the codebase `jigsaw` (served at `/jigsaw/api/**`: the jigsaws, their pieces and pictures, the users and the plays; code in `functions/src/jigsaw/`, `createJigsawApp`). What the routes share is in `functions/src/common/` (the list of documents `jigsaw-list.ts`, the document store, the allowed origins). The frontend calls it through `environment.jigsawApi`; Hosting (`firebase.json`) and the dev proxy (`frontend/proxy.conf.json`) send `/jigsaw/api` to the function.
- `functions/src/models/shared/` – **the model, the ONE place to edit shared code.** `scripts/sync-models.mjs` copies it to `frontend/src/app/models/shared/` (generated, gitignored, never edit that copy). Shared files may only import from each other.
- `client-wrappers/` – the native apps that wrap the live site, copied from Seven Friends and renamed (app id `io.karinsjigsaw.app`): `android/` and `ios/` (Capacitor), `windows/` (.NET WPF + WebView2). **All wrappers always open the play module, `https://karins-jigsaw.web.app/play` (`server.url` in `frontend/capacitor.config.json`, `AppUrl` in `windows/MainWindow.xaml.cs`; change them together).** Read `client-wrappers/README.md` first: it lists what was taken out of the Seven Friends copy (alarms, sounds, push, tray, share target, and every permission except the internet: keep it so). Icons: `npm run icons`. Scripts: `npm run android:sync`, `ios:sync`, `windows:run`, `windows:release`, `android:config`, `ios:config`.

### The model (`functions/src/models/shared/`)
A saved jigsaw is a `JigsawSpec` and is checked by `JigsawSettings`; the pieces on the board are placed objects (`PlacedFigure`) and `Puzzle` is what they do together.
| file | what is in it |
|---|---|
| `jigsaw-settings.ts` | a saved jigsaw as a document (`JigsawSpec`: name, size, grid, difficulty, line and colours, owner, visibility, timed) and `JigsawSettings`, which checks one |
| `jigsaw-kind.ts` | the kind of an entry (`JigsawKinds`): a ready jigsaw (what a document that says nothing is) or a **picture** (only the stored picture; the pieces and the difficulty are chosen in each round), and the size of a picture (`pictureSize`) |
| `jigsaw.ts` | the cut into pieces (`JigsawCut`), `JigsawPieces`, `pieceGrid`, limits |
| `play.ts` | a round (`Plays`, `PlaySpec`): positions, settings of the player, time played; a round of a picture also has its own cut, difficulty and pieces (`Plays.jigsawFor`, `startOwn`, `choiceError`) |
| `popularity.ts` | what makes a jigsaw popular (`Popularity`: rounds played and being new) |
| `jigsaw-user.ts` | the users who play (`JigsawUsers`) |
| `placed.ts`, `puzzle.ts`, `geometry.ts`, `base-object.ts` | placed objects (the pieces are placed objects), `Puzzle`: what they do together (snap, layers, groups, collecting pieces), points, boxes and the part of a picture a frame shows, the settings of an object (`BaseObject`) |

## Rules for the code
1. **Classes work on their own object.** A rule belongs on the class that owns the data, at the right level of the parent/child hierarchy: what every object has is on `BaseObject`, where a piece is and what its place means on `PlacedFigure`, what the pieces do together on `Puzzle`. A new kind of object overrides small hooks instead of adding `if (kind === …)` branches. Placed objects know their own geometry; the puzzle does what they do together.
2. **Model objects never change.** Moving or any other change gives a new object (or a new puzzle, or the same one when nothing changed). Components keep the plain stored data in a signal and make the classes from it when they need them (`Puzzle.from(...)`).
3. **Components are thin.** A component turns what the person does into calls on the model classes and shows the result. Business rules do not live in components or in the routes.
4. **What is stored is decided by the model.** `X.stored(...)` says which fields are saved (only the known ones); `X.fromStored(...)` reads a document that an older version saved and gives it what it lacked. Routes only call them. Never save a field that is not known.
5. **One validation, used everywhere.** `error()` on the classes (`BaseObject.of(data).error()`, `JigsawSettings(...).error()`) is used by the forms, the page and the API, so they cannot disagree.
6. Keep files small and about one thing; no unused code (check with `tsc --noUnusedLocals`); comments say *why* in plain words.
7. Always use Bootstrap in pages; custom CSS only if absolutely necessary, and then in `styles.scss` (inline styles only where a value is computed, such as a position or size from the data).
8. Never edit anything in a `lib/` or `dist/` directory (compiled output): edit the `.ts` source and rebuild.
9. Do not guess when debugging: establish the mechanism (exact line/condition) before fixing; if it is not found, ask for evidence.
10. **Stored pixels, drawn at a scale.** Sizes and positions in the data are stored pixels and never depend on the screen. The board draws the pieces area at `scale` (`jigsaw-board.ts`: the picture and the space round it fitted to the room, whole and in proportion, at most 1, at least 0.1), and every pointer position is turned back with `/ scale()` before it reaches the model. The store frame is beside the picture, or below it on a screen narrower than `--app-store-below` (1200 px, `stacked`); this is only how it is drawn: the store is then a short strip (`STACKED_STORE_ROWS` grid steps high) that scrolls sideways only, the tall store cut into bands side by side; `place()` and `areaPoint()` map between a stored place and its place in the strip. Beside the picture the frame scrolls up and down only: a store wider than the frame is shown in slices one under the other (`sliceCount`), the same kind of mapping. The stored coordinates of the pieces never change. New pointer code must do the same; the handles divide their size by `--app-scale` so they stay finger-sized.
11. **Small screens.** Below 1200 px (`--app-sidebar-fold`, `$bp-xl`) the menu (`.app-navbar` of `jigsaw-shell.ts`) is at the bottom of the screen, sticky there (the shell is a column, the page `order: 1` and the menu `order: 2`; a page that fills the screen leaves room for it with `footerTop()` of `core/theme.ts`; the list of languages opens upward), and the jigsaw sidebar is folded away and opens over the board with the **Details** button in the jigsaw menu (a click anywhere but on its own controls closes it) (`JigsawHelper.sidebarOpen`, kept in the session as `jigsaw.sidebarOpen`). Under `lg` (992 px), where the work menu of a round (the helper bar) is at the top and the menu at the bottom, the logo with the little house that says it takes you home is the first thing of the top bar (the way back to the front page; `jigsaw-shell.ts`); there is no logo bar of its own above the page.
12. **Every page remembers what the person last chose.** A choice of the person's own on a page (a view, a tab, a switch, a selection, a filter, where a floating frame was put, the defaults of a new jigsaw) is kept on the device in `localStorage` with `readPreference`/`writePreference` (`core/preferences.ts`; the value is checked when it is read, storage that is missing or refuses means the page starts with its default) and used when the page is opened again, also another day. Kept: the list (`jigsaw.list`: list or cards, the contributor, whether the choice of contributors is open), the user page (`jigsaw.userTab`, `jigsaw.userFilter`), the helper of a round (`jigsaw.helper`, `jigsaw.edgesOnly`, `jigsaw.helperFrame`, `jigsaw.helperScale`), the table the pieces lie on and the sound of a click into place (`jigsaw.table`, `jigsaw.sound`, `PlayLook`), the size and font of the text (`jigsaw.textLook`, `TextLook`), the sizes of the picture tiles and of the small labels (`jigsaw.tileSize`, `jigsaw.labelSize`, `PartSizeChoice`; a new grid of tiles in the app takes the class `tile-grid`, a small label is a Bootstrap `.badge`), whether the tiles show their details (`jigsaw.tileDetails`, `TileDetails`; a detail on a tile has the class `tile-details`), the button "Rounds" of the front page and its choices (`jigsaw.roundsList`, `jigsaw.roundsView`), in the app the page that was open last in each section (`play.last.info`, `play.last.mine`, `play.last.publish`), whether the band of the rounds going is folded (`jigsaw.roundsFolded`: folded, the band is gone and only a hairline with its badge on it is left; the badge is a white pill with a blue ring, its size follows the screen, `--app-fs-band`), the defaults of a new jigsaw (`jigsaw.newDefaults`: picture or ready jigsaw, size, number of pieces, difficulty, timed, line and colours; not the name, picture, description or author, which belong to that jigsaw), what was chosen for the last round of a picture (`jigsaw.roundDefaults`: number of pieces and difficulty), whether the install hint of the front page is closed (`jigsaw.installHint`), the language (`jigsaw.language`), and who plays (`jigsaw.userId`). Not kept, on purpose: what covers the page (the open sidebar `jigsaw.sidebarOpen` is for the session only, in `sessionStorage`), the Debug and Cheat switches, and what is typed for one jigsaw. A new choice on a page is kept in the same change that adds it.

13. **Layers (z-index) are in bands, set in `styles.scss` as `--app-z-*` tokens: no other number in a template or a component.** `0 – 99` the page and its content (Bootstrap's `z-1` … `z-3` lift one thing over its neighbours); `100 – 199` what is round the page (the sticky bands `--app-z-band`, the bars `--app-z-bar`); `200 – 899` **the play**: the working area of a round (the yellow area, `.board-root`, `--app-z-play`) and all that is in it, which is a stacking context of its own and only orders itself inside (`--app-z-pieces`, `--app-z-controls`); `900` and up **the overlays, always over the play**: menus and popups that open (`--app-z-overlay`, Bootstrap's dropdown is 1000), the sidebar opened over the board (`--app-z-sidebar`), the helper picture (`--app-z-helper`), the dialogs (`--app-z-dialog`; Bootstrap's modal, popover, tooltip and toast are above 1050). A bar that has a menu open is lifted over the play (`:has(.dropdown-menu.show)`), since the menu is part of the bar. A new popup or overlay takes a token from the top band; a new part of the play takes none (it lives inside the play).

14. **A bar stays on ONE line, and the words in it are shown only while everything fits on one line.** The menu and the helper bar of a round carry `appLabelFit` (`core/label-fit.ts`): it measures, not guessing from the screen width. If the bar wraps, the words of its buttons are hidden (`fit-1`: the icons stay, the words stay for a screen reader; `.fit-label`, `.nav-label` in `styles.scss`), and if it still wraps, what can go too (`fit-2`: the name beside the logo, and the clock of a round, marked `fit-drop`). A new bar that must stay on one line takes the directive and puts `fit-label` on the words that may go. (The app's menu as a bar does not use it: across the top or bottom each icon has its word under it.) Everything written in a menu or a bar has one text size, from the common class `menu-style` (in `styles.scss`: the normal text size for the links, the switches, the clock, the name and the buttons): put it on the bar, not a size on each text.

## Shared Firebase project (IMPORTANT)
The Firebase project `sevenfriends` is shared with Seven Friends. Therefore:
- Deploy only `hosting:jigsaw` and `functions:jigsaw` (the functions codebase; the npm `deploy*` scripts do; `npm run deploy:jigsaw` is the site alone). Never `firebase deploy` everything, and never deploy `firestore`/`storage` rules or indexes from here: that would overwrite Seven Friends' live ones. `firestore.rules` and `storage.rules` here are for the emulator only. Change live rules/indexes from the Seven Friends repo. What the jigsaw needs is written in this repo's `firestore.indexes.json`, `firestore.rules` and `storage.rules`; `npm run sync:firestore` (`scripts/sync-firestore.mjs`) READS the live indexes and rules from Firebase, MERGES in what the jigsaw lacks (it only adds, never changes Seven Friends' entries; rules go in a marked `BEGIN jigsaw` block before the live catch-all deny) and with `--apply` DEPLOYS the result straight to the project, touching no repo. No option shows the difference, `--check` exits 1 when live lacks something. Indexes need the logged-in firebase CLI; rules need a service account (`GOOGLE_APPLICATION_CREDENTIALS` or `--key <file>`). This is the one allowed way to change live rules/indexes from here, and only with `--apply` when asked; a later deploy from the Seven Friends repo can replace the jigsaw's block, so run it again after that. A new query that needs a composite index is added to this repo's `firestore.indexes.json` first.
- The functions codebase is `jigsaw`, not `default`, so a deploy here never deletes Seven Friends' functions.
- Collections (`jigsaw`, `jigsaw-users`, `jigsaw-favorites`, `jigsaw-config`, `jigsaw-shares`) and Storage paths (`jigsaw/...`) are the jigsaw's own, so they cannot collide with Seven Friends' data. **The one exception is `user-account`**, Seven Friends' collection of accounts, which the sign-in with Firebase (`logons/`, `docs/guide/users-and-logon.md`) writes to the way Seven Friends' own `POST /users/sync` does (the same fields, only what a login proves) and never anything else of it.
- Do not run any `firebase` command that changes the shared project, and do not deploy, commit or push, unless asked.

## Running and testing
- `npm run install:all`, then `npm run dev` (functions watch + emulators + frontend). The frontend is on **http://localhost:4210**; emulator ports are offset from Seven Friends (UI 4010, Functions 5011, Firestore 8090, Auth 9109, Storage 9209, Hosting 5010) so both can run together.
- `npm test` runs the backend tests (Vitest). `npm run test:emulator` starts a Firestore emulator (test ports, not the dev ones) and runs the real stores against it (`functions/test-emulator/`). The model has the real tests: every rule above is tested on the classes (`functions/test/`), the Firestore code with a small fake collection. A rule is not done until it has a test.
- `npm run build` builds both. The frontend build also copies the model (`sync:models`).
- **Version of the web app** (`major.minor.build`, e.g. 1.0.12): `scripts/sync-version.mjs` keeps the build number in `frontend/build-number.json` (committed; change major or minor there by hand) and **adds one at every frontend build** (`prebuild`: `npm run build`, `android:sync`, every deploy that builds); a dev server or test only writes the generated, gitignored `src/environments/version.ts` (marked dev) and `public/version.json`. The version is shown at **Help > About**. `core/app-update.ts` (`AppUpdate`, started in `app.ts`) asks for `/version.json` (Hosting sends it with no-cache) when the app opens, when the person comes back to it and every 10 minutes, and loads the web app again when the live version is newer (at once at start and on return; while working, when the person opens another page; once per version per tab, so it cannot loop; never on a dev server). The Android and Windows apps show the live site, so they follow it; their own versions (`build.gradle` `versionCode`/`versionName`, `KarinsJigsaw.csproj` and the installer `.iss`) are separate and only change when a new APK or installer is made.
- The frontend has no unit tests: check pages in the browser (the dev server must be the current one; restart it if the page shows old code).
- **Scroller membership check:** `scripts/board-scroller-membership.js` (run in the browser on a round) drops one piece at many points over and round the scroller and says if the page's answer to "is it in the scroller" (`belongsToStore`) differs from what the screen shows (rule 3, whole inside; along the way the scroller scrolls only the store's own length counts); run it above and below 1200 px. Empty list is good.
- **Board reach check:** `scripts/board-reach-sweep.js` (run in the browser on a round, as a user who has one) takes a piece from the store and one from the picture to every side of the board and lists jumps, disappearances, pieces outside the board and room a piece cannot reach; run it in windows of several sizes (store below the picture under 1200 px, beside it from 1200 px; a tall picture also gives the free space beside the picture). `npm run test:reach` is the same idea as a Karma spec (`jigsaw-board.reach.spec.ts`, headless Edge/Chrome, `WINDOW=1400x800`) but is not reliable yet.


## What "the jigsaw" and "the picture area" mean
The jigsaw module's page (`/jigsaw`) is called **Karins jigsaw** (the menu link, the title of the browser tab and the title at the top of its sidebar; the address `/jigsaw` and the collection `jigsaw` stay as they are). The pieces are put together on the **picture area** of the board (`JigsawBoard`); a rule in the guide files that says "the jigsaw" is about the saved document and its page, and one that says "the picture area" is about the part of the board the picture is on.
