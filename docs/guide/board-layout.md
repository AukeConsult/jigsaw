# The board: layout, helpers and the store

Part of the project guide (moved out of `CLAUDE.md`; text unchanged). Read this before changing this area.

## The rules of the board (they cannot be negotiated)

There are three areas: the **yellow** working area (where the pieces are drawn, `.pieces-layer`), the **blue** board (the picture area) and the **pink** scroller (the store frame).

1. **Pieces move freely inside the yellow area and can settle anywhere.** ONLY the edges of the yellow area govern where a piece can move and where it stays: not the blue area, not the scroller, not the gaps.
2. **Pieces join anywhere in the yellow area, except in the pink scroller; a piece goes to its own place only in the blue board area** (`Puzzle.dropPieces`: `within` is the picture area, `unseen` the hidden pieces). Only a drop of a piece that was really moved does it; the game never joins pieces by itself.
3. **A piece belongs to the pink scroller when it is fully inside it**, and only then (decided as it is dropped, `areaPoint`, and kept in its place: see "Where a piece is saved"); partly inside it is a free piece lying over it.
4. **The yellow working area is always above its surroundings** (the menu of the app, also when it is a bar stuck to an edge), so the pieces always show on top; and **anything that opens over it is above it**: the z-index bands are in rule 13 of `CLAUDE.md` and the `--app-z-*` tokens of `styles.scss` (the play 200 – 899, overlays 900 and up).
5. **A view is set up in this order:** first find the yellow area, then place the blue board in it, and last place the pink scroller.
6. **The yellow area must not go below the footer** (the menu of the app when it is stuck to the bottom of the screen): it ends where the footer begins (`footerTop()` in `core/theme.ts`, used by `measureArea`).
7. **The yellow area always fills the height, down to the footer, and is measured again whenever the screen, the page or the footer changes size** (`measureArea`: the window, the visual viewport, a rotation, a resize of the page, and the menu being stuck to an edge or taken away from it, which tells the window it was resized: the area is made as high as is left down to the real top of the footer). **Where the scroller is below the board (a narrow window) it is only 2 pieces high** (`STACKED_STORE_ROWS`, its scroll bar under them) and lies against the bottom edge of the yellow area (`stripExtra`); what is between the board and the scroller is free space. Pieces are free anywhere in it, and in the scroller only when the whole of them is inside what it shows.



- **Helper banner and hidden picture:** once the picture is cut into pieces (the jigsaw has pieces) the picture is no longer shown behind the picture area (`backdrop` of the board: it is derived, nothing is stored; the pieces still show their parts of it). The menu of the puzzle has a switch, **Whole picture** (on to begin with, kept on the device as `jigsaw.helper`; `JigsawHelper`; the board shows the frame only with its input `helperBanner`). When it is on, the whole picture is shown in a frame that floats on top of the rest of the page (`position-fixed`): it is moved by its title (`helperFrame`, kept on the window; the pointer is captured by the title, which is not redrawn), sized by its corner (`helperScale`: a quarter of the picture's width and height to begin with, from a tenth up to a half, the picture keeps its shape) and closed with its x (which turns the switch off).
- **The border round the picture area uses the piece line settings** (`JigsawSpec.lineWidth` 0-6 px and `lineColor`, the same as the line round each piece): the board draws it as an `outline` (outside the picture area, so it takes no room and nothing placed moves) when `lineWidth` is more than 0. `JigsawSpec.frameWidth` (and `JigsawSettings.DEFAULT_FRAME_WIDTH`/`MAX_FRAME_WIDTH`) is no longer set or drawn by the pages; it stays in the model so older jigsaws still load. The form of a picture keeps `lineWidth`, `lineColor` and `backColor` when it saves (a save replaces the whole document).
- The band of the rounds going on the home page has a border in the blue, green and yellow of the pattern (`.my-rounds.ongoing`).
- **The helper choices are kept on the device** (`core/preferences.ts`, `localStorage`: kept when the page is reloaded, left and come back to, and another day; no storage, or a value that is not what it should be, means the board starts as if nothing was kept): the two switches (**Whole picture**, **Edges only**) and the place and the size of the helper picture frame (`jigsaw.helper`, `jigsaw.edgesOnly`, `jigsaw.helperFrame`, `jigsaw.helperScale`). They are not saved in the database. The panels over the puzzle begin closed, since they cover the board, and the Cheat switch is not kept.
- **The pieces are drawn in a layer of their own, on top of both frames** (the board's `overlay` and `place()`): with a store, every piece is drawn in one layer that lies over the picture area and over the store frame, and not inside either, so a piece is never cut off or put under a frame, also when it lies over the edge of one (a group of pieces that stick together can lie over the picture area and the store). The layer is as big as the whole area (so it scrolls with it), takes no room, lets the pointer through except at the pieces, and is above the frames (`z-index: 1`; the handles and the picking frame are above it). A piece of the store is put where the store frame shows it (the board follows how far the frame is scrolled, `storeScroll`), is cut off only at the top and bottom of the frame (`clip-path`, not at the sides), and is left out when it has been scrolled out of sight. Without a store the objects lie in the picture area. The frame itself is only the scrolling background and the place a frame is dragged on to pick pieces.
- **Helper: Collect pieces:** a click on the empty store frame puts the pieces that lie in the scrolling store frame together at the top of it (`Puzzle.collectPieces`, called by the board with the pieces in the store that are shown: with "only corner and edge pieces" on, the hidden ones are not collected). All the pieces of the store are taken (the whole scroll area, every band, not only what is shown). When the store scrolls up and down they are packed in rows from the top left of the frame (`COLLECT_MARGIN` from the top and the sides, `COLLECT_GAP` between, both shares of the size of a piece), in the order they were lying: from the top down, and in a row from left to right; a row that is full starts the next one. **When the store is a strip below the picture that scrolls sideways** (`stacked`) the strip is filled in the direction it scrolls (`Puzzle.collectPieces` with `across: {bandHeight, stride}`): the pieces are taken in the order they lie across all the bands (left to right, from the top down in a column) and put column by column from the left end, a column as high as a band, and when a band is full the next band goes on at the right of it. **When the store is beside the picture and wider than the frame** (it is shown in slices as wide as the frame, one under the other) the pieces are put in rows inside the first slice and, when it is full, in the next one (`down: {sliceWidth, sliceHeight}`), so no row is split over two slices; the store is made as wide as the slices the pieces took (it is only ever made wider). **The same check also runs by itself** (`JigsawBoard.bringBackStrays`, an `effect` in the constructor): 0.6 s after what decides the visible places (the window, the store, the free space) or where the pieces are has stopped changing, and not while a piece is held, not in a round that is only looked at, and not before the area is measured, the pieces that strayed (it happens when the window is resized) are put back in the store, only they, with nothing scrolled. **An overall check goes with it** (`Puzzle.strayPieces`, with the places a piece can be seen and reached from `JigsawBoard.visiblePlaces`: the working area and the store): a piece whose middle is in none of them (the working area and the store) is brought back with the rest, whole groups, never a group on its place; the hidden "edge only" pieces are left. Pieces that stick together are put as one block and keep their places in relation to each other; a group that has a piece outside the store (or hidden) is not collected. The frame is scrolled to its top. It changes only places (they are saved like any move of pieces); the button is disabled when there is nothing in the store.
- **Helper: only corner and edge pieces:** a second switch of the menu, **Edges only** (off to begin with, kept on the device), hides the pieces from the inside of the picture, so the border can be put together first. A border piece is one with a flat side (`JigsawPieces.isBorder`: a corner has two, an edge piece one), which is every piece with `col` or `row` on the border of the picture. What is hidden is only not drawn: it stays where it is, is saved as before, and is not picked by a frame dragged over it (`hiddenIds` of the board).
- **Where a piece is saved** (`placed.ts`: `STORE_LEFT`, `STORE_SPACE`, `PlacedFigure.inStore`): **a piece on the board is saved where it is seen**, in stored pixels from the top left corner of the picture area, anywhere in the working area (the picture, its margin, beside and under it, over the scroller). **A piece in the store is saved from `STORE_LEFT` (100000) across**, far from every place on the board, and the board shows it where the store frame has it (slices or bands, scrolled). So two pieces that look side by side are side by side in what is saved, and nothing on the board is ever taken for something in the store. Which of the two a piece is in is decided once, as it is dropped (`areaPoint`). A group is all in one of them.
- **A smaller screen** (`settledPuzzle`, `freeArea`, `Puzzle.confine`): a free piece on the board that the working area does not show is pushed into view, its group whole; it is saved with the next move. The push never joins pieces (`Puzzle.pushed` moves a group that would fit a pixel further). The pieces of the store are left to the store.
- **How wide the store frame is on the screen (beside the picture):** at most `STORE_MAX_PIECES_ACROSS` (2.5) pieces wide plus its scroll bar (`storeFrameWidth` in `jigsaw-board.ts`), so the picture gets all the room that is left (the scale is fitted to the picture, the gap and the narrow frame). It lies against the right edge of the board: when the picture is limited by its height there is room left over and the frame is moved right by `frameExtra` (the places of the pieces in it add that move, `place()`). The scroll bar (5 px, `STORE_SCROLLBAR_PX`) lies against the right edge and the board has no padding there (nor under the strip when the store is below the picture), so a piece can go up to the bar. A wider store is shown in slices one under the other (`sliceCount`).
- **The margin round the picture, and what belongs to the store:** the picture lies `areaMargin(width, height)` in from the top and left edge (**relative to the size of the picture area**: a twentieth of its shorter side, never less than 20 px; `AREA_MARGIN_RATIO`, `AREA_MARGIN_MIN_PX` in `placed.ts`, `Puzzle.margin`, `margin` of the board) of the pieces area, and a piece may go into that margin too (`PlacedFigure.fits`, `Puzzle.moveFigure`, `moveGroup` and `settled` all allow a box to begin that far before 0; the API checks the same), so a piece moves freely over the whole area. The store frame (the scroller) is its own place: a piece is in it only when the whole of it is inside what the frame shows (beside the picture: its middle is half a piece in from the frame's left edge and, while the store is not scrolled, from its top edge, and half a piece from the right end of what is shown, the scroll bar not counted; `areaPoint`, `view`), and nothing of the frame (its margin, where it was moved to) is used to place a piece that is not in it: such a piece lies on the board whole outside the frame (its middle at least half a piece from the frame's left edge), and a piece dropped with its middle nearer the frame's edge goes back out to there.
- **The three areas and what they do:** (1) the area the pieces are drawn in (the layer, yellow in the Cheat lines; a small margin, `ROOT_PADDING_PX`, lies round it): a piece moves freely over all of it and settles wherever it is let go; (2) the picture area (blue): a piece (or a group) goes to its own place only when its middle is inside it (`Puzzle.dropPieces` with `within`); neighbours join anywhere on the board; (3) the store frame (the scroller, pink): a piece belongs to it only when the whole of it is inside what it shows (`areaPoint`), and nothing joins there; lying partly over its edge, its top or its scroll bar it is a free piece, drawn over the frame. Nothing of the frame's margin or place is used to place a piece that is not in it.
- **Rule 4, layers:** the working area of a round (`.board-root`, `--app-z-play`, 200) is over the bars (120) and the sticky bands (110), so the pieces always show on top; every overlay is over it (`--app-z-overlay` 1000 for a menu that opens from a bar, the panels over the puzzle 1010, the helper picture 1040, the dialogs 1060). Inside the play only `--app-z-pieces` (1) and `--app-z-controls` (2) are used, as the play is a stacking context of its own.
- **The order a view is set up in:** first the yellow working area is found (`room`, `areaHeight`: what the page gives it, less its small margin), then the blue picture area is placed in it (`scale`, fitted to the room that is left when the narrow scroller has taken its width, the margin `areaMargin` at the top and the left), and last the pink scroller is placed (`storeFrameWidth`, `storeViewHeight`, `frameExtra`: against the right edge, or below the picture). Nothing that is placed later changes what was placed before it, and a place that is found is never moved by one that is placed after it.
- **The rule that cannot be negotiated:** inside the working area (the yellow one) ONLY its edges limit where a piece can move and where it stays; the blue area, the store frame and the gaps do not. Two things only depend on the other areas: a piece goes to its own place only when it is dropped on the blue picture area, and it belongs to the store frame only when the whole of it is inside what the frame shows (where it also joins nothing). How this is kept: a free piece is saved where it is seen; the held piece is kept inside the layer's rectangle (`boardBounds`). Checked by dropping a piece at 81 points over the working area at 1500×900 (frame next to the picture) and at 2400×1300 (frame moved right): it stays exactly where it is let go. Not yet so with the store below the picture (narrow window): a piece let go on the 8 px of padding at the ends of the strip counts as in the strip.
- **The store frame:** a jigsaw that has been cut has a `storeWidth` (saved with it, `JigsawSettings.storeFrame`): the store, `STORE_HEIGHT_FACTOR` (3) times the height of the picture area high and that wide, drawn as a frame beside the picture area (below it on a narrow screen), scrolling. The cut puts the pieces in it, scattered (see "Jigsaw pieces" in `pieces-and-play.md`). Every place a piece can have (`JigsawSettings.extent`, used by the board and the API to check that every object fits) reaches from the board to the end of the store. While a piece is moved the pointer is held by the whole board, not by the piece, since the piece is drawn again when it changes between board and store; over the frame the pointer position is the frame's (scroll included), elsewhere it is on the board, and not outside what the working area shows. A rectangle that picks pieces keeps to the one it was begun in. A piece has no resize handles.
- **A jigsaw is given a number of pieces, not a grid size:** the form of a ready jigsaw offers the standard numbers, whole columns x rows in the shape of the size chosen (`CutChoice`, `JigsawCut.pieceChoices`); picking one makes the picture area fit it exactly (`JigsawCut.layoutOf`), and the form tells the grid size that follows. It is always worked out from the standard size that was chosen, not from the size the picture area was made last time, so choosing other numbers of pieces and back gives the same picture area again. Many at once asks for about a number of pieces (`JigsawCut.layoutFor`: the pieces are square, a piece is about the square root of (width × height / pieces) on a side, the grid size that gives the number nearest to the one asked for is taken, and the picture area is made a whole number of pieces wide and high, so the number can come out a few more or fewer). The grid size is still what is saved (`gridSize`); the number of pieces shown is columns × rows. Pieces that are already cut keep the size they were cut with until the picture is cut again.
- The grid of a new jigsaw is 50 px (older documents without a grid read as 5 px).
- The jigsaw board puts its grid points half a step in from the edge (the first row and column are half a step from the border, as the last are from the far one; objects snap to those points: the centre sticks to 25, 75, 125 ... for a 50 px grid, `origin` of `Puzzle.moveFigure`), keeps its width and height a whole number of grid steps (`JigsawSettings.fittedSide`, applied when a size or the grid is changed) so the last row and column of points are as far from the far edge as the first are from the near one, numbers the points 1, 2, 3 ... row by row, and so do the pieces, but only when the **Cheat** is on (a switch in the Details panel, off by default; not when the points are closer than 20 px), and has no list of objects at the side and no title, hint text or name/size line above the picture area. Its **jigsaw pieces area** (jpa: the picture area with its store frame, where a piece can be moved anywhere) fills the screen of the puzzle page, down to the footer (`fillScreen`, measured again on resize and scroll); the details are a panel over it.


- **After Collect the scroller is as long as its pieces** (`JigsawBoard.storeFitted`, `storeUsed`, `storeInnerSize`): once the store has been collected (a click on it, or the Edges only switch) its scroll length is only as long as the pieces in it reach, one piece of room after the last (down beside the picture, across below it), and never less than the frame shows, never more than the store was cut. It is worked out from the places of the pieces (as `place` maps them), so a resize cannot leave it out of date, and nothing of it is saved.
- **A piece that is picked up is drawn whole:** the cut at the edge of the store frame (`clip` of `place`) is for a piece lying in the frame; a held piece (`BoardView.held`) has none, so it is never cut off wherever it is taken.
