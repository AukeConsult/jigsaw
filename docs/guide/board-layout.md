# The board: layout, helpers and the store

Part of the project guide (moved out of `CLAUDE.md`; text unchanged). Read this before changing this area.

- **Helper banner and hidden picture:** once the picture is cut into pieces (the jigsaw has pieces) the picture is no longer shown behind the picture area (`backdrop` of the board: it is derived, nothing is stored; the pieces still show their parts of it). Above the jigsaw pieces area there is a one-line helper banner (`helperBanner` of the board) with a switch, "Helper picture" (on to begin with, not saved). When it is on, the whole picture is shown in a frame that floats on top of the rest of the page (`position-fixed`): it is moved by its title (`helperFrame`, kept on the window; the pointer is captured by the title, which is not redrawn), sized by its corner (`helperScale`: a quarter of the picture's width and height to begin with, from a tenth up to a half, the picture keeps its shape) and closed with its x (which turns the switch off).
- **The border round the picture area uses the piece line settings** (`JigsawSpec.lineWidth` 0-6 px and `lineColor`, the same as the line round each piece): the board draws it as an `outline` (outside the picture area, so it takes no room and nothing placed moves) when `lineWidth` is more than 0. `JigsawSpec.frameWidth` (and `JigsawSettings.DEFAULT_FRAME_WIDTH`/`MAX_FRAME_WIDTH`) is no longer set or drawn by the pages; it stays in the model so older jigsaws still load. The page for playing keeps `lineWidth`, `lineColor` and `backColor` when it saves (a save replaces the whole document).
- **The two helper switches are also kept for each jigsaw** (`jigsaw.helperByJigsaw`, a map of jigsaw id to `{helper, edgesOnly}`; `JigsawHelper.open(id)` is called by the round page: a jigsaw opened before shows its switches as they were left, a new one keeps the last choice). The band of the rounds going on the front page has a border in the blue, green and yellow of the pattern (`.my-rounds.ongoing`).
- **The helper choices are kept on the device** (`core/preferences.ts`, `localStorage`: kept when the page is reloaded, left and come back to, and another day; no storage, or a value that is not what it should be, means the board starts as if nothing was kept): the two switches ("Helper picture", "Only corner and edge pieces") and the place and the size of the helper picture frame (`jigsaw.helper`, `jigsaw.edgesOnly`, `jigsaw.helperFrame`, `jigsaw.helperScale`). The open sidebar of a narrow screen is kept for the session only (`core/session-storage.ts`), since it covers the board. They are not saved in the database; the Debug button is not kept.
- **The pieces are drawn in a layer of their own, on top of both frames** (the board's `overlay` and `place()`): with a store, every piece is drawn in one layer that lies over the picture area and over the store frame, and not inside either, so a piece is never cut off or put under a frame, also when it lies over the edge of one (a group of pieces that stick together can lie over the picture area and the store). The layer is as big as the whole area (so it scrolls with it), takes no room, lets the pointer through except at the pieces, and is above the frames (`z-index: 1`; the handles and the picking frame are above it). A piece of the store is put where the store frame shows it (the board follows how far the frame is scrolled, `storeScroll`), is cut off only at the top and bottom of the frame (`clip-path`, not at the sides), and is left out when it has been scrolled out of sight. Without a store the objects lie in the picture area. The frame itself is only the scrolling background and the place a frame is dragged on to pick pieces.
- **Helper: Collect pieces:** a click on the empty store frame puts the pieces that lie in the scrolling store frame together at the top of it (`Puzzle.collectPieces`, called by the board with the pieces in the store that are shown: with "only corner and edge pieces" on, the hidden ones are not collected). All the pieces of the store are taken (the whole scroll area, every band, not only what is shown). When the store scrolls up and down they are packed in rows from the top left of the frame (`COLLECT_MARGIN` from the top and the sides, `COLLECT_GAP` between, both shares of the size of a piece), in the order they were lying: from the top down, and in a row from left to right; a row that is full starts the next one. **When the store is a strip below the picture that scrolls sideways** (`stacked`) the strip is filled in the direction it scrolls (`Puzzle.collectPieces` with `across: {bandHeight, stride}`): the pieces are taken in the order they lie across all the bands (left to right, from the top down in a column) and put column by column from the left end, a column as high as a band, and when a band is full the next band goes on at the right of it. **When the store is beside the picture and wider than the frame** (it is shown in slices as wide as the frame, one under the other) the pieces are put in rows inside the first slice and, when it is full, in the next one (`down: {sliceWidth, sliceHeight}`), so no row is split over two slices; the store is made as wide as the slices the pieces took (it is only ever made wider). **The same check also runs by itself** (`JigsawBoard.bringBackStrays`, an `effect` in the constructor): 0.6 s after what decides the visible places (the window, the store, the free space) or where the pieces are has stopped changing, and not while a piece is held, not in a round that is only looked at, and not before the area is measured, the pieces that strayed (it happens when the window is resized) are put back in the store, only they, with nothing scrolled. **An overall check goes with it** (`Puzzle.strayPieces`, with the places a piece can be seen and reached from `JigsawBoard.visiblePlaces`: the picture area with the room under or beside it, the free space beside the picture, the store): a piece whose middle is in none of them (it strayed when the window was made smaller or the store was changed) is brought back with the rest, whole groups, never a group on its place; the hidden "edge only" pieces are left. Pieces that stick together are put as one block and keep their places in relation to each other; a group that has a piece outside the store (or hidden) is not collected. The frame is scrolled to its top. It changes only places (they are saved like any move of pieces); the button is disabled when there is nothing in the store.
- **Helper: only corner and edge pieces:** a second switch in the helper banner, "Only corner and edge pieces" (off to begin with, not saved; disabled when there are no pieces), hides the pieces from the inside of the picture, so the border can be put together first. A border piece is one with a flat side (`JigsawPieces.isBorder`: a corner has two, an edge piece one), which is every piece with `col` or `row` on the border of the picture. What is hidden is only not drawn: it stays where it is, is saved as before, and is not picked by a frame dragged over it (`hiddenIds` of the board). The banner tells how many are shown of how many.
- **The free space beside the picture is a place for pieces too** (`JigsawBoard.zone`, only when the store is below the picture, `stacked`, and the room beside the picture is at least three grid steps wide): a piece is either in the **store** (its centre at `centerX >= store.left`: the strip below the picture) or **free** on the board. A free piece can be anywhere in the picture area and in the empty room beside it, and moves with the pointer without a jump from the one to the other, also over the gutter above the store, and down into the store and back. Nothing new is stored: the room beside the picture stands for the stored free space under the picture (`base` = the picture height + a grid step + half a piece; `origin` is half a piece before the picture area ends), so the stored places stay within the model's area; `place()` draws such a piece beside the picture, `areaPoint()` turns the pointer there back to a stored place (for a piece, the store starts half a piece below the top edge of the strip, so a piece is whole inside the store), and the `puzzle` computed pulls a free piece that is not inside what is shown of it back in (`Puzzle.confine`), so a piece is never outside the window. A piece that is fixed on its place stays where it is. **A piece that is moved is kept on the board** (`onBoard`: not outside the pieces area, and, with the store below the picture, not below the store: dragged further down it stops at the bottom of the store and does not jump). **When the window is made smaller** and there is no room beside the picture any more, the free pieces that lay beside or under the picture are pulled up to the picture and the gutter above the store (`Puzzle.confine` in the `puzzle` computed), so none is outside what is shown. With the store beside the picture nothing of this is used (the store frame and the free space have no colour of their own: the pieces lie on the board).
- **The store frame and the jigsaw pieces area (jpa):** a jigsaw that has been cut has a `storeWidth` (saved with it, `JigsawSettings.storeFrame`): a frame beside the picture area, one grid step from its right edge, `STORE_HEIGHT_FACTOR` (3) times the height of the picture area high, scrolling up and down. The cut puts the pieces in it, scattered (see "Jigsaw pieces" in `pieces-and-play.md`). The picture area and the frame together are the jpa, where a piece can be moved anywhere: all of it is one coordinate area (`JigsawSettings.extent`, used by the board, the page and the API to check that every object fits), and a piece with its centre right of the picture area is drawn in the frame. While a piece is moved the pointer is held by the whole board, not by the piece, since the piece is drawn again when it changes place; over the frame the pointer position is the frame's (scroll included), elsewhere it is on the part of the jpa left of the frame, and not outside what the area shows. A piece can be moved anywhere on the jpa: on the picture, in the blank space under the picture (as far down as the area shows) and in the store frame. A piece has no resize handles.
- **A jigsaw is given a number of pieces, not a grid size:** the sidebar asks for "Pieces" (2 to 1000; the standard numbers are whole columns x rows in the shape of the picture area, `JigsawCut.pieceChoices`, and picking one makes the picture area fit it exactly, `JigsawCut.layoutOf`) and tells the grid size that follows (`JigsawNew.relayout`, `JigsawCut.layoutFor`): the pieces are square, a piece is about the square root of (width × height / pieces) on a side, the grid size that gives the number of pieces nearest to the one asked for is taken, and the picture area is made a whole number of pieces wide and high (so the number can come out a few more or fewer, and the box shows the number that really comes out). It is worked out again when the width, the height or the number of pieces is changed (when the field is left), always from the size the person typed (`askedSize`), not from the size the picture area was made last time, so asking for other numbers of pieces and back gives the same picture area again. The grid size is still what is saved (`gridSize`); the number of pieces shown on opening is columns × rows. Pieces that are already cut keep the size they were cut with until the picture is cut again.
- The grid of a new jigsaw is 50 px (older documents without a grid read as 5 px).
- The jigsaw board puts its grid points half a step in from the edge (the first row and column are half a step from the border, as the last are from the far one; objects snap to those points: the centre sticks to 25, 75, 125 ... for a 50 px grid, `origin` of `Puzzle.moveFigure`), keeps its width and height a whole number of grid steps (`JigsawSettings.fittedSide`, applied when a size or the grid is changed) so the last row and column of points are as far from the far edge as the first are from the near one, numbers the points 1, 2, 3 ... row by row, and so do the pieces, but only when **debug** is on (the Debug button in the sidebar, off by default; not when the points are closer than 20 px), and has no list of objects at the side, no title, hint text or name/size line above the picture area, a **sidebar**: the settings panel (with the title "Karins jigsaw" at its top) is docked at the left edge, 17 rem wide and as high as the visible screen under the top menu (`sidebar`, measured again on resize and scroll), and the pieces area fills the rest, right under the top menu, and its **jigsaw pieces area** (jpa: the picture area with its store frame, where a piece can be moved anywhere) is as high as the visible screen, from its top to the bottom of the window (`fillScreen`, measured again on resize and scroll).

- **The free space beside the picture uses all the room beside it** (`JigsawBoard.zone`, `columns`, `stride`; `JigsawSettings.freeSpaceTop`, `freeSpaceColumnHeight`, `freeSpaceBottom`, `FREE_SPACE_COLUMNS_MAX` = 12): the places it stands for are under the picture and only as wide as the picture, so a room wider than the picture is cut into **columns**, each as wide as the picture and one under the other in the stored places (column `k` begins `base + k * height` down; it is drawn `k * stride` further across). Each column begins one piece before the end of the one before (`stride` = the width of the picture minus a piece), so a piece moved from one column to the next follows the pointer all the way, with no jump. The area is as high as the lowest column (`JigsawSettings.extent`), which only lets more places in (what is saved stays valid). `areaPoint`, `place`, the pulling in of free pieces (`Puzzle.confine`, one call for each column) and the check for strays (`visiblePlaces`) all use the columns. Resizing recalculates the room, the columns and the places first; the check of the pieces comes after (it waits 0.6 s for them to settle).
