# Pieces and playing

Part of the project guide (moved out of `CLAUDE.md`; text unchanged). Read this before changing this area.

- **The cut of a picture is made in the round** (`Plays.startOwn`: `JigsawCut.layoutOf` makes the picture area a whole number of pieces, `JigsawCut.cut` cuts it, the pieces are kept under the round); a ready jigsaw is cut when it is made. After that a piece is a piece: moving, snapping, sticking and saving work the same, with the round's grid and difficulty (`Plays.jigsawFor`). The helper picture is drawn cover-fit (`object-fit: cover`), since the picture area of a round can be a few pixels off the shape of the picture; the pieces already use `preserveAspectRatio="xMidYMid slice"`.
- **Position:** a piece's *centre* is what sticks to a grid point; a piece close to its own point clicks into place by the difficulty. Everything stays inside the picture area and the store.
- **Layers:** pieces are on layers 1–1000; a higher layer is in front.
- **Saving:** every change saves by itself a pause later (autosave); nothing is saved while the settings are not allowed. A saved change **replaces the whole document** (only the creation time is kept), so nothing removed or stale is left in the database.
- **Jigsaw pieces** (`functions/src/models/shared/jigsaw.ts`, `JigsawCut`): a ready jigsaw is cut when it is saved with its picture (the form of a picture, or many at once): one piece for each whole cell of the grid (at most `JIGSAW_MAX_PIECES` = 1000 per jigsaw, so use a bigger grid for a big picture; `JIGSAW_MAX_PLACED` = 500 is for the other objects of a jigsaw). A piece is an ordinary placed square figure of grid × grid pixels with `piece` (`col`, `row` and its four edges: 1 tab, -1 blank, 0 flat on the border); a piece has no resize handles. Every inner edge is a tab on one piece and the matching blank on the other, chosen by chance. The pieces start scattered in the store frame (`JigsawCut.scatter`: in rows, with a random space between neighbours across and down from a little overlap (-0.3 of a piece) to 0.8 of a piece, `PIECE_GAP_MIN`, `PIECE_GAP_MAX`, so pieces can lie partly over each other, and each on a random layer; the store is made as wide as the pieces need). A new round, and **Reset round**, shuffle the pieces again (`JigsawCut.reshuffle`: new places and layers in the store frame, the same edges; what is not a piece stays). `app-puzzle-piece` draws a piece: its outline (`JigsawCut.path`, the tabs reach outside its square) cut out of the picture, which covers the whole picture area, so a piece shows its own cell of it wherever it lies; a matched piece has a green outline. The number of the grid point a piece belongs on is drawn on it only when the **Cheat** is on.
- **A piece can never be lost** (`Puzzle.settled()`, used by `dropPieces` and by the board for everything it draws): every piece that is fixed (on its own place, or stuck to one that is) is on the bottom layer (`FIXED_LAYER` = 1) and every free piece is above it (layer 2 and up), so a free piece is never under a fixed one (and the pieces are drawn in a layer of their own over the picture area and the store frame, so never under them either); a free piece whose box lies outside the area is pulled back in; and a border piece is also known from its cell (`JigsawPieces.isBorder(piece, grid)`: column or row on the border), not only from its flat sides, so "only corner and edge pieces" never hides one. Old layers and places are put right when a game is drawn, nothing has to be saved for that.
- **Pieces that stick together form a group** (`Puzzle.stuckGroup`): two pieces stick when they are neighbours in the picture (next to each other, or one over the other) and lie exactly where they belong in relation to each other (one piece apart; places are whole or half pixels, so it is exact, `STICK_TOLERANCE_PX`); a piece sticks to what its neighbour sticks to, so it is a whole group. **Nothing is stored for a group: it follows from where the pieces are**, so a saved jigsaw that is opened has the same groups, and the saving of piece places (`PieceSync`) is all it needs. Picking up a piece takes its whole group with it (`moveGroup`; the board works out the pieces that move, `moving.ids`, when it is picked up); a picked frame of pieces takes the groups of all of them. **A bond is never broken** (logically or visibly): there is no way to pull a piece loose (Shift does not), a group is moved, pulled back into the area (`Puzzle.settled`) and into the free space (`Puzzle.confine`) whole, by the same amount for every piece, it is drawn by the piece that stands for it (`Puzzle.groupAnchors`, the held piece), and the server refuses (409) moves that would pull apart pieces that stick together (`Plays.bondsError`). Only Reset (all pieces are scattered anew) ends groups. **Dropping** (`Puzzle.dropPieces`) is where pieces stick: a group that is dropped within the snap range (`snapPercent`, the same setting as for clicking into place: 0 means nothing sticks) of its own grid point, or of the place where one of its pieces fits a neighbour that is not in the group, moves by the smallest of those steps, so that piece is exactly there, and the groups that now fit each other are one group. It clicks exactly or not at all: when the area has no room for the whole step the group stays where it was dropped. Only the piece that was dropped blinks (not the pieces it joined, not the whole group) and the device vibrates. Reset scatters all the pieces anew, and so ends every group.
- **Playing:** a piece is moved in steps 50 times smaller than the grid (`PIECE_STEP_DIVISOR`, made a whole number of half pixels, since the server only takes a whole or a half pixel for the place of a piece, and not finer than half a pixel), not from grid point to grid point, and it does not snap while it is moved. A piece that is picked up goes to the front (`Puzzle.bringToFront`: a layer above all the others) and stays there when it is dropped. **Several pieces can be picked and moved at once**: drag a frame on the empty picture area or the empty store frame (it works in both), then drag any picked piece and all move by the same amount (`Puzzle.moveGroup`: half pixels, as far as all stay inside the area; a group keeps its order when it goes to the front). **A piece clicks into place only when it is dropped** within a range of its own grid point (its home, `PlacedFigure.home()`, the centre of its cell of the picture): it goes exactly there (`Puzzle.dropPieces`, called by the board when what is moved is let go), **blinks** (swells, shakes and shines for 0.7 s: the animation is the small CSS of `app-puzzle-piece`, kept to this) and the device **vibrates** (`navigator.vibrate`, where it can). **How close it has to be is a game parameter, `snapPercent`**, a percent of the grid size, saved with the jigsaw document (`JigsawSpec.snapPercent`, a whole number from 0 to 100, where 0 means a piece never clicks; 50, half a grid step, when the jigsaw has none, `JigsawSettings.DEFAULT_SNAP_PERCENT`; `JigsawSettings.snapRangePx` makes pixels of it), (piece line: `lineWidth` 0-6 px and `lineColor` #rrggbb on the jigsaw, `JigsawSettings.DEFAULT_LINE_WIDTH/COLOR`, drawn by `.piece-outline` through `--app-line-width/-color`) set as the Difficulty (Hard 10 %, Medium 25 %, Easy 40 %; `jigsaw/snap-level.ts`) in the form of a ready jigsaw, and for each round of a picture in `app-round-choice` (not changed in a round). Pieces that are in place when a jigsaw is opened or shuffled do not blink, nor do pieces that were in place when they were picked up. **A piece that lies on its own place is fixed** (`PlacedFigure.inPlace`, `Puzzle.isFixed`): it can be picked but never moved, and neither can the whole group of pieces that stick to it (`Puzzle.movable`, and `moveFigure`/`moveGroup` change nothing for them; `collectPieces` leaves them). A group with no piece on its place moves all together. Moves that wait for the 0.8 s pause are sent at once when the page is closed or hidden (`PieceSync.leaving`, a `keepalive` request), so the game is as it was left.

- **Pieces that click to each other also click to the grid (`Puzzle.dropPieces`):** a group that is dropped clicks to its own place or to a neighbour (the smallest step within `snapRange`). If it clicked to a neighbour, what it is now one group with is looked at again as a whole: when that whole group is within `snapRange` of its own place it clicks to the grid too, so pieces that stick to each other are not left a little off the grid when the piece they stuck to was itself a little off its place. A group that is not within range of its own place stays where the click put it. Only on the picture area (rule 2 of the board).

## How the play area looks and sounds
Only looks and sound: where a piece can move, where it stays and what it clicks to are as before (the five rules of the board).
- **Depth** (`styles.scss`, classes the board puts on `.placed-object`: `is-loose`, `is-single`, `is-held`): a piece that is not in its place casts a soft shadow (two layers of its own outline, drawn in `puzzle-piece.html`, no filter, so many pieces cost little); a piece **on its own** is also turned a little (at most `TILT_MAX_DEG`, from its id, so always the same) and, held, straightens, grows and casts a bigger shadow; a group is never turned or grown; a piece in its place lies flat.
- **The table is the theme of the whole app** (the account's preference; `play-main[data-table]`, painted once on the body behind the pages and the menu, which are see-through; a dark theme is veiled with white on the pages so they can be read; a puzzle has its round's table as it is; the admin and the pages of signing in are not themed). **Everything that has a background or a colour follows it** (in `styles.scss`, at the tables): the band of the rounds going, the welcome band, the header and edge of the floating menu, and the text (names and titles with an effect of their own, authors, counts, links, icons, buttons, ticks). Jigsaw is the app's own look. A new theme gets its table, its swatch and its row of text colours; a new place with a background is added to `$themed-places`.
- **Tables** (`PlayLook`, `jigsaw/play-look.ts`: `jigsaw.table`, jigsaw to begin with): plain, jigsaw (the app's own soft pattern and colours), felt, wood or linen, or a season (spring, summer, autumn, winter: blossoms, waves and sun, leaves, snowflakes over its colour), or a mood (calm: still ripples; angry: bolts on deep red) on the working area, drawn by the stylesheet (no picture files). The table runs on into a menu stuck to an edge (the page is painted once, behind the see-through bar and board; light words on the dark ones). The board (picture area) is a mat a little lighter than its table, and so is the store; a jigsaw with a background colour of its own keeps it. Plain is the white it was.
- **A click into place** (the same moment the piece blinks): a ring of light spreads from the piece and a short sound is made by the browser (`board/snap-sound.ts`, deeper the bigger the group), unless the player switched it off (`jigsaw.sound`).
- **Whose choice it is:** a preference of the account (`playLook` on the account, set on `/play/account/preferences`, and kept on the device for nobody and before the account is read). **A round can have its own, stored on the round** (`PlaySpec.look`, `PATCH /:playId/look {look | null}`, the player only; kept when the round is reset), over the preference: the entry **Table** of the puzzle menu opens a panel over the puzzle (`app-round-look`; "Use my preferences" takes the round's own away). **A device that asks for less motion gets no turning, growing or ring.**
- **The shape of the tabs** (`CutShapes`, `models/shared/cut-shape.ts`): classic (what says nothing), round, pointed, square or wave. The pieces stay squares on the grid, so snapping, grouping and the border pieces are the same for all; **each piece carries its shape** (`piece.shape`), and `JigsawCut.path` draws it, so the board, the store and the example need nothing else. An outline must look the same from either end (a tab fills the blank of the neighbour, which runs the edge the other way) and reach out about a quarter of a side at most. Chosen at the start of a round of a picture (kept with the round, `cut.shape`, and on the device) and in the form of a ready jigsaw (`cutShape`; a change cuts it again, and not once rounds are played). Many pictures at once cuts classic.
