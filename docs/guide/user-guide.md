# Using Canvas

The app has two modules, each with its own menu at the top. **Karins jigsaw** (`/jigsaw`) is the one the app opens with: a jigsaw puzzle made from a picture. **Canvas** (`/canvas`) is for other purposes and for testing: it has the drawing board (**Canvas area**) and **Objects** (the shapes you can put on the canvas). A small link in each menu goes to the other module. Everything saves by itself a moment after a change ("All changes saved" shows under the settings). Nothing is saved while a setting is not allowed; the page says why.

## Canvas page
- **Settings:** name, width and height in whole pixels, grid (pixels between grid points, 0 = no grid) and a switch that shows or hides the grid points. The grid size is kept when the points are hidden.
- **Saved canvases:** click one to open it, **New canvas** to start another, **Delete** to remove one for good.
- **Object list:** the objects made on the Objects page, to the side of the canvas. Drag one onto the canvas to place a copy.

### Placing and moving
- A placed object is an independent **copy**. Changing or deleting the original object never changes a canvas.
- The **centre** of an object sticks to a grid point. Everything stays inside the canvas.
- Drag an object to move it. Click to pick it; a picked object shows its handles. **Delete** (or Backspace, when you are not typing in a field) removes the picked objects.

### Figures and arrows
- A **figure** (square, circle, triangle, histogram) has 8 connection points on its border (a triangle has 6). They show when you pick it or while you drag something.
- An **arrow** has two connection points, one at each end.
- Drag an arrow end close to a figure point (within 24 px) and it **snaps** onto it exactly and is fastened: the point is drawn as a point in a point. The other way round works too: drag a figure close to a free arrow end.
- A fastened arrow **follows** its figure when you move or resize it; the other end stays. Deleting the figure frees its arrows.
- Grab an arrow near an end to move that end alone. The two round handles on its sides change its **width across**.

### Curving an arrow
A picked arrow shows three diamond handles, a quarter, half and three quarters of the way along it. Drag each sideways on its own to curve the arrow, even into an S. Drag back to the line to make it straight (3 px or less on all three is straight). The ends do not move, so what the arrow is fastened to stays fastened.

### Resizing
- **One figure:** drag one of its 8 handles. The opposite edge stays, the edge goes to the grid, a square or circle stays equal-sided, and it is at least 4 px.
- **Several objects:** drag a rectangle on the empty canvas to pick everything inside it, then drag a handle of the box. Positions scale with the box on each axis; each figure keeps its shape and scales by the factor that changed more. An arrow is stretched by its ends and its thickness and curve scale the same way.

### Layers
The canvas is layer 0. Objects are on layers 1–1000, the first on 10. A new object goes on the highest layer in use. A higher layer is in front. Change the layer of the picked objects in the layer field.

## Karins jigsaw (the jigsaw page, jcanvas)
Works like the canvas page, with its own saved list (collection `jigsaw`), and with these differences:
- The grid points are half a step in from the edge; the **Debug** button in the sidebar shows or hides their numbers (1, 2, 3 … row by row; off to begin with); the width and height are kept a whole number of grid steps, so the last row and column of points are as far from the edge as the first. The default grid is 50 px.
- **Background picture:** choose a jpeg, png or webp; a window opens with the picture behind a frame of the shape of the canvas: drag the picture inside the frame to find the cut and zoom with the slider or the mouse wheel, then press **Use this cut** (Cancel keeps the old picture). The cut picture is made at most about 5 MB and kept in the database (in Storage). The helper picture shows the cut picture. If you change the width or height of the canvas later, choose the picture again to cut it to the new shape. **Remove the picture** takes it away.
- **Cut into pieces:** replaces what is on the canvas with jigsaw pieces, one for each grid cell. The pieces start in the **store**, a frame beside the picture that is three times as high as the picture, with the pieces scattered in it (close together, some overlapping, in a random front-to-back order) and a scroll bar to reach them all. With **Debug** on, each piece shows the number of the grid point it belongs on in the picture. A piece clicks into place only when you **drop** it close enough to its own place (the grid point with its number): it goes exactly there, blinks, and your device vibrates (if it can). How close is the **Snap range (% of the grid)** in the sidebar: 50 % (half a piece) to begin with, saved with the jigsaw, and 0 means a piece never clicks. You give the number of **Pieces** instead of a grid size: the sidebar works out the grid size (and makes the canvas a whole number of pieces wide and high), and tells you what it came to. **Pieces stick together:** drop a piece (or a group) close to where it fits a neighbour in the picture, within the snap range, and it moves exactly into place and sticks to it; the pieces that joined blink. Pieces that stick form a group that you move around together: take any piece of it and all come along. Hold **Shift** while you take a piece to pull that piece loose from its group. Drag a frame on the empty canvas or the empty store to pick several pieces, then drag any of them to move all at once. **Reset** shuffles all the pieces again, on new random places and layers. The picture and the store together are the **jigsaw pieces area**: drag a piece anywhere on it, from the store onto the picture and back; it moves in very small steps (50 times smaller than the grid), not from grid point to grid point. The area is as high as the visible screen. Everything is saved with the jcanvas: every piece is kept in the database with its number, where it is, on which layer and whether it is matched, so when you open a saved jigsaw the game is as you left it. The sidebar shows how many pieces are matched (**Matched: x of y**), and a matched piece has a green outline.
- After the cut the picture behind the canvas is hidden, so the pieces make the picture. A banner above the pieces area has a **Helper picture** switch. When it is on, the whole picture is shown in a frame on top of the page, a quarter of its size to begin with. Drag the frame's title to move it, drag its bottom right corner to make the picture bigger (up to half its size) or smaller (down to a tenth); the x on the frame hides it.
- A **click on the empty pieces frame** (a press that is let go without dragging) does the same as Collect pieces. Dragging a rectangle there still picks pieces.
- The helper banner has a **Collect pieces** button: it puts the pieces that lie in the scrolling store together at the top of it, close to each other but not in tidy rows (each piece lies a little more by chance, and the distances differ), and scrolls the store to its top, so you do not have to hunt for them. (Pieces that stick together are collected as one block.) The helper banner also has an **Only corner and edge pieces** switch: when it is on, the pieces from the inside of the picture are hidden, so you can put the border together first. A hidden piece is only not drawn: it is still where it was, and is saved as before. The banner tells how many pieces are shown. When you make a jigsaw you also choose the **Frame (px)**: how thick the blue frame around the picture area is, so you can see where the picture is built (4 to begin with, 0 for none). What you choose in the helper (the two switches, and where and how big the helper picture is) stays for the session: it is as you left it when you reload the page or come back to it in the same browser tab, and starts over when the tab is closed.
- **Fits every screen:** the pieces area is drawn smaller when the screen is small, so the whole picture is always in view in its own proportions (the pieces, the grid and the frames shrink with it). The store sits beside the picture with the picture taking 2/3 of the width of the page and the store frame the rest (the frame scrolls **up and down only**: when the store is wider than the frame, it is shown cut into slices as wide as the frame, one under the other, so the pieces are arranged inside the view and spread again when the window changes size), on a screen 1200 px wide or more; on a narrower screen the store goes **below the picture** instead: the picture stays fixed at the top, and the store is a strip below it that goes on down to the bottom of the screen and scrolls **sideways only**. When you change the size of the window the strip changes height with it, and the pieces are spread over its bands again. The tall store is shown cut into bands as high as the strip, laid side by side, so you scroll along to see all the pieces (only how it is shown: the stored places do not change). Dragging a frame to pick pieces in the strip works within one band. The whole area down to the bottom of the screen is the jigsaw pieces area: you can drop a piece anywhere on it, also on the empty space below the strip, where it stays where you leave it. Only how it is shown changes: the pieces keep the places they are stored at, so the same jigsaw looks right on every screen. It never grows bigger than the real size and never goes below a tenth of it. Nothing in the jigsaw depends on the screen: the same jigsaw can be opened on a phone and on a big screen. The helper picture is a share of the size the picture is shown at.
- **On a screen under 1200 px wide** the sidebar is folded away behind a bar above the pieces area that shows the name and "Matched x of y"; press the bar to open the sidebar over the area and again to close it. Which you chose stays for the session.
- There is no list of objects at the side.

## Objects page
Make and edit the objects you place on canvases.
- **Figure:** shape, default size (drag to draw the size), border width and colour, background and front colours, font size, corner radius (rounded corners).
- **Connector (arrow):** one way or both ways, solid or hollow. A hollow arrow's thickness is its border and you drag its size; a solid arrow's height is its thickness.
- A square and a circle always have equal sides. Objects autosave too, and can be deleted from the list.

## Limits
Canvas sides 1–10000 px, name up to 60 characters, at most 500 placed objects per canvas, objects at most 10000 px on a side, 100 canvases and 200 objects saved.
