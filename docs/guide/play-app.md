# The play module (`/play`): the new app

Part of the project guide. Read this before changing anything in `frontend/src/play/`. The short version is in `CLAUDE.md` ("The app and its routes"); this file explains the module as a whole.

## What it is
`/play` is **the new app**, started over from nothing in `frontend/src/play/`. It lives beside the existing website (`/jigsaw/...`, `frontend/src/app/jigsaw/`), which stays and is refactored later. Both use the same backend, the same model (`functions/src/models/shared/`) and the same colour scheme, backgrounds and size scale. **The native wrappers (Android, iOS, Windows) always open `https://karins-jigsaw.web.app/play`**, never the website (`server.url` in `frontend/capacitor.config.json`, `AppUrl` in `client-wrappers/windows/MainWindow.xaml.cs`; change them together).

What a person does in it: look at the pictures, press one to put it together (a **round**), add pictures of their own, look at their pictures and rounds, change their account, and read the info about the app.

## Two modes
The device has a mode, `play` or `jigsaw` (`core/app-mode.ts`, kept as `app.mode`). Opening anything under `/play` sets `play`, anything under `/jigsaw` sets `jigsaw` (`modeGuard`). The top routes follow the mode: home (`/`) goes to `/play/list` in play mode, and a top route such as `/list` goes to `/play/list`. What is unknown under `/play` goes to the home of the mode.

## The rules of the module
1. **Components and services are shared with the website, pages never are.** The app frames the website's components (`app-puzzel-board`, `app-my-jigsaws`, `app-jigsaw-header`, `app-welcome`, `app-guide-document` and so on) in pages of its own. When a website page holds what the app needs, the content is first made a component, and the page of the website frames it too. Components have an `inApp` input where their links must lead into `/play/...`.
2. **One component, framed into different pages**, so the person sees the same layout each time: the form of a picture (`play-picture-form`) is framed by Add picture and by the page of a picture.
3. **Every route carries a page and the menu that goes with it** (`data.menu`, a function of who plays, in `helper/menus.ts`, read by `play-route.ts`).
4. **The look is `styles.scss` only**: the five text sizes (`--app-fs-*`), the button sizes, the colour tokens, the page width `--app-page-max` (75 rem). Nothing sets a size or a font of its own (see `look-and-texts.md`). Every page is at most `.page-max` wide, except the puzzle, which fills the screen.
5. **Texts** are the scope `play` (`frontend/public/i18n/<language>/play.json`), in all nine languages like the rest of the app (`npm run check:i18n`); reused scopes (`jigsawNew`, `jigsawList`, `guide` ...) are translated too. The language is the account's, else the one chosen on the device, else the browser's if the app has it, else English; it is chosen in the last row of the home page menu. The texts of the tiles of the home page come from the backend and are English for now.
6. **Every choice the person makes is kept on the device** (`readPreference`/`writePreference`, rule 12 of `CLAUDE.md`): the list filter, the switches of the menu, whether the menu is open, where it was put.

## The frame: main page and helper
- **`PlayMain`** (`play-main/`) is the main route. It fills the screen and is **the one thing that scrolls** (the window itself never does). It has one `<router-outlet>` (the page that is open), the admin band (`app-admin-banner`, when an admin is in another account) and the frame. It has the soft puzzle-piece pattern behind the pages, as the website has (not behind the puzzle).
- **`PlayFrame`** (`play-frame/`) lies fixed over the page and everything on it and takes no clicks itself. It holds the helper. (There is no button of its own for the info pages: Info is an entry of the page menu.)
- **The helper** (`helper/helper.ts`) is **one movable menu on the main level**. It shows the menu of the open page: a list of entries, each an icon, words and the page a press goes to, or a **switch** (`toggle`) of the page that is open. A press is plain routing (`routerLink`): there are no helper views and no helper subclasses.
  - **The first entry of every menu is the name of the app** (the logo with the little house: it goes home, `/`). The first row has two small buttons at its right end: the arrow that folds the menu to that row and opens it again, and the little arrow into a corner, which folds the menu and puts it in the top right corner (a double click on the row does the same). The first row is the same in every menu, sized by one parent class (`.play-helper .brand-item`).
  - It is dragged with the pointer, kept inside the window, grows from the side nearest to the edge when it opens or its words change, and its place and whether it is open are kept on the device. Until it is moved it is in the bottom right corner.
  - **Dragged to an edge of the window it sticks there as a bar** (top, bottom, left or right; the pointer within 2 rem of the edge; `.play-bar`, the second form in `helper/helper.html`). Across the top or bottom the entries are side by side on one line (`appLabelFit` with `fitSome`: **as many entries as there is room for have their words**, from the start of the bar; when there is no room even for the icons, the name beside the logo goes too). **A switch has no little control in a bar: its icon is white on the colour of the app when it is on**; down a side they are one under the other, and on a narrow screen only the icons. The language is a button whose list opens over the page, away from the edge. **The arrow at the far end collapses the bar** (it points to the edge; `play.helperBarFolded`, apart from whether the floating menu is folded): only a small tab with the logo and the arrow that opens it is left at the start of the edge, over the page, and no room is kept for it. A bar has no corner arrow. **The page makes room for the bar** (`--app-play-bar-top`, `-bottom`, `-left`, `-right`, set by the helper; it also tells the window it was resized, so the board of a puzzle measures its room again, and `footerTop()` knows a bar at the bottom). **The room a bar has left says "Drag to move"** in tiny letters (`.play-drag-hint`, next to the edge of the bar that faces the page: the lower one of a top bar, the upper one of a bottom bar, the right one of a left bar, the left one of a right bar; across the top or bottom, down a side upright one under the other; all of it or none when there is no room; not on a collapsed bar). **A bar flows together with the page**: the same soft pattern (`background-attachment: fixed` on both, so there is no seam), no line and no shadow; on the puzzle, which has no pattern, the bar is plain too. A bar stays on its edge until the pointer is dragged away from it; dragged away it is the floating menu again. The edge is kept on the device.
  - **The last row of the home page menu is the language** (`app-language-select`, the menu entry `language: true`, shared with the website: a row with the flag and name of the language in use, which opens the list of languages one step down, each with its flag; the choice is kept on the device and as the account's language). Until a language is chosen the language of the browser is used when the app has it.
  - Switches that have nothing to switch are not offered (Ongoing rounds without rounds going, My favorites without favorites). A finished puzzle has no help picture and no border pieces to switch.

## The home page is a list of tiles
The list at `/play/list` (and the website's finder on its front page) shows **tiles**, not only pictures. `FeedItem` (`functions/src/models/shared/feed-item.ts`) is the parent class: `PictureItem` (made from a search result) and `KeptItem` with `InfoItem`, `OfferItem` and `PackageItem`. Pictures come from the search (`play-cards` asks it, with the filters and the endless scroll as before). The other tiles come from `GET /jigsaw-feed` (`FeedService`), which reads each kind's own collection and gives the tiles that are right and active now (`FeedItems.shown`): this is the one place where a rule of who is shown what will be added. `HomeFeed.merge` puts them among the pictures, by their place counted in pictures from the first one, so a page of pictures loaded later never moves a tile that is already there; a tile whose place is not loaded yet waits. The app and the website use the same service, the same rule and the same tile component (`app-feed-tile`).

**Information, offers and packages are written by a publisher or an admin** (`/play/mine/info`, `/play/mine/offers`, `/play/mine/packages`; a user menu entry each): a list with a search, a filter on the status (upcoming, active, expired) and a row for each tile with Edit and Delete, and a form to add or change one. A publisher has their own, an admin every tile (`FeedItems.mayChange`). Each kind has its own collection (`jigsaw-info`, `jigsaw-offers`, `jigsaw-packages`), its own routes (`/jigsaw-info`, `/jigsaw-offers`, `/jigsaw-packages`, `feedManageRouter`) and a date it is shown from and a date it expires. **The words are in every language**: the author writes in one language, the master language, and **the AI makes the other eight when the tile is saved** (`Translator`, `functions/src/jigsaw/translator.ts`; what it used is kept for the account that saved, `token-usage`, kind `translate`). An entry an author corrects by hand is never overwritten; the AI entries are made again when the master words change (`FeedItems.toTranslate`: the fingerprint of the master words, `sourceHash`). What is shown is the language that is chosen, else the master's, field by field. If the AI cannot be asked the tile is saved with its master words only and the page says so.

**Public, private or hidden.** Every tile says who sees it (the same choice as a jigsaw, `app-visibility-choice`): public (what none says), **private** (only its owner sees it on the home page, **with a clear banner on it, so it is not forgotten and left private**: it is for checking how it looks) or hidden (nobody sees it on the home page; it is only in its owner's list). **A new tile is private to begin with.** `GET /jigsaw-feed` takes who asks from the header (`FeedItems.shown(documents, now, viewer)`).

**A package** is like an offer (the words in every language, dates, visibility) and it **has a price or is free** (`price` in cents and `currency`; free is what none says), **is in categories** (the same as a picture's, at most three, chosen with `app-category-picker`; **the person's search on the home page shows a package only when it matches the chosen categories and the search words**, `FeedItem.matchesFilter`; information and offers are not content and are always shown) and **holds pictures**. The pictures are ordinary pictures in the same collection as all the others (`jigsaw`), each linked to the package by `packageId` (`JigsawSpec.packageId`; only the package's owner, or an admin, can put a picture in it); **they are not in the general list or the search** until a person has the package (below). The detail of a package (`/play/mine/packages/:id/pictures`) shows its pictures as cards, a press on one opens the page of the picture ready to be changed (`/play/mine/picture/:id?edit&package=`; a link on top, Back to the package, and Delete go back to the package, and a change never takes the picture out of it: the API keeps `packageId`), each can be deleted, and **many files are uploaded at once** (the website's `app-jigsaw-batch` in its mode `packageId`: only pictures, each linked to the package). A package that has pictures cannot be deleted until they are (the list deletes them first, after asking). **A package on the home page is a tile with a random picture of its collection** (the answer names up to eight ids, chosen at random each time: `coverIds`, with `pictureCount`), the title, whether it is free or its price, and how many pictures it holds; pressing it opens the page of the package (`/play/package/:id`: its words, price and all its pictures).

Its owner (or an admin; they must still be a publisher) has **Edit the package** on top of that page, which opens its form (`/play/mine/packages/:id`).

**Only a verified account adds a package** (`users-and-logon.md`).

**Having a package.** On top of the page of a package is **Add to my pictures** (a free one) or **Buy** (a priced one; **nothing is charged: payment is not built**, and the page says so). The package is then kept under the account, in the subcollection `jigsaw-users/<id>/bought-packages` (`BoughtPackages`, `bought-package.ts`: the price it had, and `paid: false` for a priced one), by `PUT /jigsaw-users/:id/bought-packages/:packageId` (only the account itself, or an admin; only a package that is active and that the account may see). **From then on the pictures of the package are in that account's search, and the account may open and play them**; nobody else finds, opens or plays them but their owner and an admin (`BoughtPackages.mayUse`, asked by the search, one jigsaw, the lists and the rounds). **My pictures lists the packages the account has** (My packages, tiles above the pictures, only while there are any; `GET /jigsaw-feed/mine`, also a package that is not on the home page any more), but not their pictures one by one (decided: the search is enough): its list holds only what the user made. **In the home list a picture of a package has a tag** in the bottom left corner of its card with the name of the package (`.package-tag`; just Package when the home page has that package no more), which leads to the package; the cards on the page of a package have none. Until a person has it, the page of the package shows its pictures with a lock: they can be looked at, not opened. What was got stays on its page for that person also when the package is not on the home page any more.

The pages of the packages (`/play/packages` and `/play/package/:id`) have a menu of their own, `packagesMenu`: the way home, My pictures and All packages (nobody: home, All packages and Logon).

**All packages** (`/play/packages`, in the page menu, also for nobody, and linked from the top of each package) shows every package there is to get as tiles; the ones the account has are marked In my pictures. **The home page does not list a package the account has got** (`GET /jigsaw-feed` says `had`, and `HomeFeed.merge` leaves out what is not `onHome`): its pictures are in the list, each with the tag that leads to it, and All packages has it too.

## The puzzle page
The puzzle page does not scroll: it is as big as the screen. The details lie beside the board on a wide screen and over it on a small one (a click anywhere but on their own controls closes them). The working area reaches the top of the screen and the picture lies in from its top and left edge by `areaMargin` (a twentieth of the shorter side of the picture area, at least 20 px). A finished puzzle has the band on top with the time. The rules of the board are in `board-layout.md`, `rules-of-a-round.md` and `pieces-and-play.md`.

## Testing and running
There are no unit tests for the frontend: check pages in the browser (`npm run dev`, the frontend on http://localhost:4210/play). The model and the API have the real tests (`npm test`). After a change run `npx tsc -p tsconfig.app.json --noEmit --noUnusedLocals` in `frontend/`.
